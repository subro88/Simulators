📖 Mathematical Theory & Physics Derivations

WBSCTE Applied Physics-I (BS103/BS107): Rotational Kinematics, Work-Energy Theorem, and Flywheel Moment of Inertia.

1. Work-Energy Theorem & Frictional Dissipation

When a mass $m$ falls through height $h$, its loss of gravitational potential energy $mgh$ is converted into:

  • Rotational kinetic energy of the flywheel: $K_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$
  • Translational kinetic energy of falling mass: $K_{\text{trans}} = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} m (\omega r)^2$
  • Work done against bearing friction during $N$ cord turns: $W_{f1} = N \cdot W_f$
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2 + \frac{1}{2} m (\omega r)^2 + N W_f$$

2. Friction Energy Loss per Rotation

After the cord detaches at $N$ turns, the flywheel continues coasting for $n$ rotations against friction until coming to rest. Thus, all its remaining kinetic energy is dissipated into friction work over $n$ turns:

$$n W_f = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2 \implies W_f = \frac{1}{2n} I \omega^2$$

3. Final Analytical Expression for $I$

Substituting $W_f$ back into the energy equation and solving for $I$:

$$I = \frac{N m g h}{N + n} \left( \frac{t^2}{4\pi^2} \right)$$

Where the maximum angular velocity at detachment is $\omega = \frac{4\pi n}{t}$.

📚 Comprehensive Experimental Manual & Guidelines

1. Objective & Physical Principles

To determine the moment of inertia $I$ of a flywheel disc and measure the frictional torque $\tau_f$ of its axle bearings using a two-stage experimental procedure: Stage 1 Vernier Caliper measurement of axle radius $r$, followed by Stage 2 dynamic gravitational potential energy release.

2. Step-by-Step Experimental Procedure
  1. Stage 1: Close Vernier Caliper jaws to determine instrument zero error. Clamp jaws onto flywheel axle shaft to measure MSR and VC. Record $\ge 3$ trials and validate mean axle radius $r$.
  2. Stage 2: Wind $N$ turns of cord carrying mass $m$ around the axle shaft. Set height of fall $h$.
  3. Click Release Mass & Start Flywheel. Observe cord uncoiling and mass descending.
  4. Upon cord detachment at $N$ turns, note maximum angular speed $\omega$ and count coasting rotations $n$ until flywheel comes to a complete stop over time $t$.
  5. Verify calculated moment of inertia $I = \frac{N m g h}{N + n} \left( \frac{t^2}{4\pi^2} \right)$.
