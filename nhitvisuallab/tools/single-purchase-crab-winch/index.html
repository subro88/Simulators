Mathematical Theory & Engineering Kinematics

1. Kinematic Velocity Ratio (\(VR\)) Formulations

The Velocity Ratio is defined as the distance moved by effort (\(y\)) divided by the distance moved by load (\(x\)) in the same time interval.

$$\text{Single Purchase Mode:} \quad VR_{\text{single}} = \left(\frac{2 R}{D_{\text{eff}}}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{T_4}{T_1}\right) = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{T_4}{T_1}\right)$$

In one complete turn of the effort handle (\(y = 2 \pi R\)), the pinion rotates once, causing the spur wheel to rotate \(\frac{T_1}{T_4}\) revolutions. The load drum of radius \(r\) lifts the load by \(x = \left(\frac{T_1}{T_4}\right) \cdot 2 \pi r\).

$$\text{Double Purchase Mode:} \quad VR_{\text{double}} = \left(\frac{2 R}{D_{\text{eff}}}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{T_4}{T_3}\right) = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{\text{Product of Teeth of Driven Wheels}}{\text{Product of Teeth of Driver Wheels}}\right)$$

2. Statics, Mechanical Advantage & Efficiency

$$MA = \frac{W}{P}, \qquad \eta = \frac{\text{Output Work}}{\text{Input Work}} = \frac{W \cdot x}{P \cdot y} = \frac{MA}{VR} \times 100\%$$

Ideal effort required to hoist the load in the absence of friction is \(P_{\text{ideal}} = \frac{W}{VR}\). The effort lost in overcoming machine friction is:

$$P_f = P - P_{\text{ideal}} = P - \frac{W}{VR}, \qquad F_f = (P \cdot VR) - W$$

3. The Law of the Machine (\(P = mW + C\))

Empirical experimental observation confirms that effort \(P\) relates linearly to load \(W\):

$$P = m \cdot W + C$$
  • Slope \(m\): Rate of effort increase per unit load (\(m = \frac{dP}{dW}\)).
  • Intercept \(C\): Zero-load friction effort required to overcome initial bearing stiction, gear teeth rubbing, and rope stiffness.

From the Law of the Machine, the theoretical maximum limits are obtained as load \(W \to \infty\):

$$MA_{\text{max}} = \lim_{W \to \infty} \left(\frac{W}{mW + C}\right) = \frac{1}{m}, \qquad \eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{MA_{\text{max}}}{VR} = \frac{1}{m \cdot VR} \times 100\%$$

4. The Self-Locking Condition (\(\eta < 50\%\))

A lifting machine is Self-Locking if it does not spontaneously overhaul or reverse when effort is removed. When \(P = 0\), the work done by the descending load must be insufficient to overcome internal machine friction, leading directly to the universal mechanical theorem:

$$\eta < 50\% \implies \text{Self-Locking (Safe)}; \qquad \eta > 50\% \implies \text{Overhauling (Back-driving)}$$

📚 Comprehensive User Guide & Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

1. Apparatus Overview & Mechanical Principle

A Crab Winch is a heavy-duty hoisting machine utilizing toothed spur gear reductions to lift massive structural and industrial loads with modest human manual effort. In Single Purchase, a single pinion meshes directly with a large spur gear keyed to the load drum. In Double Purchase, an intermediate axle carrying a compound spur wheel and pinion is engaged, providing two-stage gear reduction and massive velocity ratios (\(VR > 100\)).

2. Apparatus Calibration & Metrology
  1. Count the teeth on all gears: Driver Pinion 1 (\(T_1\)), Driven Spur 1 (\(T_2\)), Driver Pinion 2 (\(T_3\)), Driven Spur 2 (\(T_4\)).
  2. Measure the handle radius \(R\) with a steel rule from the axis of Shaft 1 to the handle grip.
  3. Measure the diameter of the cylindrical load drum \(D_{\text{drum}}\) and cord thickness \(d_{\text{rope}}\) using vernier calipers.
  4. Compute the effective diameter \(D_{\text{eff}} = D_{\text{drum}} + d_{\text{rope}}\) and theoretical \(VR\).
3. Experimental Execution & Progressive Loading
  1. Hang a base test load (e.g., \(W = 10\text{ kgf}\)) onto the load hook.
  2. Incrementally add fractional brass weights to the effort pan until the winch begins to turn smoothly and lift the load at uniform speed without jerking.
  3. Record total effort applied \(P\) (sum of weights plus 50g pan mass).
  4. Calculate Mechanical Advantage \(MA = W/P\) and Efficiency \(\eta = (MA/VR) \times 100\%\).
  5. Repeat for increasing loads (\(20, 30, 50, 80, 100\text{ kgf}\)) and log into the Observation Ledger.
4. Dog-Clutch Shifting Procedure

To shift from Single Purchase to Double Purchase, click the "Shift Dog Clutch" lever. The effort axle slides axially, disengaging the direct mesh and bringing Pinion 1 into mesh with Spur 1 (\(T_2\)), while Pinion 2 (\(T_3\)) meshes with Spur 2 (\(T_4\)). Notice that the effort required to lift the exact same \(10\text{ kgf}\) load drops by more than half.

5. Graphical Analysis & Extraction of Constants

Plot effort \(P\) against load \(W\). Perform linear regression to obtain the slope \(m\) and zero-load intercept \(C\). Compute the maximum theoretical mechanical advantage \(MA_{\text{max}} = 1/m\) and maximum efficiency \(\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1}{m \cdot VR} \times 100\%\).

6. Safety Audit: Self-Locking vs. Overhauling

Click the "Test Self-Locking (Drop Effort)" button while a heavy load is suspended. If the machine efficiency is below \(50\%\), internal bearing and tooth friction prevent back-driving, ensuring the load remains stationary in mid-air.

Apparatus Mechanics & Gear Train Theory

Lifting machines are mechanical devices designed to elevate heavy loads through the strategic application of comparatively smaller effort forces. In a single purchase crab winch, mechanical advantage is achieved via a single-stage involute spur gear pair and an effort crank radius greater than the winding drum radius. In double purchase systems, compound reduction distributes the high torque across an intermediate countershaft, enabling safe elevation of multi-ton loads.

The Universal Law of the Machine

Across all empirical regimes, the total applied effort satisfies \(P = mW + C\), where \(mW\) represents the effort required to lift the ideal mechanical load and overcome load-proportional contact friction, while \(C\) represents the stiction and viscous drag of journal bearings at zero load.
