📚 PC Maintenance & Hardware Assembly Engineering Guide

Comprehensive laboratory textbook reference covering desktop computer architecture, electrical power rail distribution, thermodynamic heat dissipation, memory topology, and motherboard diagnostic state machines.

⚡ 1. Electrical Power Budget & ATX Rail Distribution

Modern PC components draw regulated DC current across three primary voltage rails: +12V, +5V, and +3.3V, with +5VSB (Standby) powered continuously.

• +12V Rail Dominance: High-TDP modern CPUs (via 8-pin EPS) and discrete graphics cards (via PCIe 8-pin / 12VHPWR) draw over 85% of total system power from the +12V rail.
• Load Factor (ηload): Standard engineering sizing recommends sizing the PSU so peak dynamic load operates at 50%–70% capacity, where 80+ Gold/Platinum switching efficiency peaks.

🌡 2. Thermodynamic Heat Transfer & TIM Physics

Thermal dissipation across the CPU die is governed by Fourier's law and lumped-capacitance thermal resistance networks:

• Thermal Interface Material (TIM): Fills microscopic air voids between the copper IHS and cooler cold plate. Air has a very poor thermal conductivity ($k \approx 0.026\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) versus silver/silicone paste ($k \approx 5\text{ to }8.5\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$).
• Forgotten Plastic Peel Film: Retaining the factory protective plastic sticker introduces an insulating thermal barrier ($R_{\text{th}} > 0.85\ ^\circ\text{C/W}$), triggering emergency thermal throttling at $100^\circ\text{C}$ within seconds of boot.

💾 3. Dual-Channel Memory Topology & Interleaving

Modern memory controllers provide two 64-bit independent channels (or four 32-bit sub-channels in DDR5):

• Primary Pair (A2 + B2): In daisy-chain motherboard trace topologies, memory signals travel through slots A1/B1 to reach A2/B2. Populating slots 2 and 4 avoids open-stub signal reflections, ensuring signal integrity at high DDR5 transfer rates.
• Dual-Channel Bandwidth: Doubles effective memory bandwidth: $\text{BW} = \text{Clock} \times 8\text{ bytes} \times 2\text{ channels}$.

🔊 4. Motherboard POST State Machine & Q-LED Hierarchy

Upon chassis power button activation, the motherboard BIOS executes a 4-stage sequential hardware audit:

• CPU Init (Red LED): Verifies VRM power delivery, socket contact continuity, and CPU clock generator.
• DRAM Training (Amber LED): Reads Serial Presence Detect (SPD) EEPROM and trains clock-to-data memory timings.
• VGA Init (White LED): Initializes primary PCIe display controller and handshakes VESA display output.
• BOOT Device (Green LED): Handshakes UEFI NVMe/SATA storage drives and transfers control to OS bootloader.

📖 Laboratory Student Manual & Operations Guide

1. Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) Safety: Always click Toggle ESD Strap to ground yourself to the unpainted chassis frame ($V_{\text{static}} = 0\text{V}$) before installing sensitive microchips.

2. CPU & Thermal Cooler Installation: Open the CPU ZIF lever, orient gold triangle ($\blacktriangle$) to Pin 1, apply thermal paste, remove the cooler's protective peel film, and secure the heatsink.

3. Dual-Channel RAM Installation: Seat matching memory sticks into slots DIMM_A2 and DIMM_B2 until both latches click firmly into place.

4. Power Supply Cabling: Connect the 24-Pin ATX main connector, 8-Pin EPS CPU VRM cable, and PCIe 8-Pin GPU cable. Ensure front-panel `PWR_SW` is oriented to pin 6 & 8.

5. Diagnostic Verification: Turn on the rear PSU rocker switch, press the front-panel power button, and watch the Q-LED sweep sequence pass to hear 1 Short Beep (POST Pass).
