WBSCTE Laboratory Manual — Experiment #1

1. Aim of the Experiment

To determine the volume and density of a hollow cylinder by measuring its external diameter ($D$), internal diameter ($d$), and length ($L$) using a Vernier Caliper.

2. Apparatus Required

  • Vernier Caliper with metric scale (range 0–15 cm, Least Count $0.01\text{ cm}$).
  • Hollow metal cylinder specimen (Brass / Steel / Aluminum).
  • Analytical balance (for mass measurement).
  • Magnifying glass for inspecting vernier coincidence.

3. Principle & Mathematical Formulae

A Vernier Caliper consists of a Main Scale graduated in millimetres and a sliding Vernier Scale.

Least Count ($LC$): The smallest magnitude measurable by the instrument:

$$LC = 1\text{ MSD} - 1\text{ VSD} = \frac{\text{Value of 1 Main Scale Division}}{\text{Total Number of Vernier Divisions}} = \frac{0.1\text{ cm}}{10} = 0.01\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ mm}$$

Total Observed Reading:

$$\text{Observed Value} = \text{MSR} + (\text{VSR} \times LC)$$ $$\text{Corrected Value} = \text{Observed Value} - (\pm e)$$

Volume of the Hollow Cylinder Material ($V$):

Let $D$ = External Diameter, $d$ = Internal Diameter, and $L$ = Length of the cylinder.

$$V = V_{\text{outer}} - V_{\text{inner}} = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 L - \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 L = \frac{\pi}{4} (D^2 - d^2) L$$

Density of the Material ($\rho$):

$$\rho = \frac{M}{V} = \frac{4M}{\pi (D^2 - d^2) L}\text{ g/cm}^3$$

4. Precautions & Sources of Error

  1. Avoid applying excessive force on the thumb screw to prevent jaw deformation and zero error shift.
  2. Always measure diameter at three different mutually perpendicular orientations to eliminate out-of-round ovality errors.
  3. Ensure the inside measuring nibs are held parallel to the cylinder axis and rock gently to find the maximum chord (true diameter).
