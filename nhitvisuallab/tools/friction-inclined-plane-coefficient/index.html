📖 Mathematical Theory & Physics Derivations

WBSCTE Applied Physics-I (BS103/BS107): Mechanics of Limiting Friction, Angle of Repose, and Work-Energy Balance on an Inclined Plane.

1. Limiting Static Friction & Angle of Repose ($\theta_r$)

When a block of mass $m$ rests on an inclined plane making an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal, gravity exerts a downward force $W = mg$. Resolving this into rectangular components:

$$\text{Normal Component: } N = mg \cos\theta$$ $$\text{Down-Plane Gravity Component: } F_{g\parallel} = mg \sin\theta$$

At limiting equilibrium (the instant the slider is on the verge of slipping), the static friction reaches its maximum value $f_s^{\text{max}} = \mu_s N$. Balancing forces along the incline:

$$mg \sin\theta_r = \mu_s mg \cos\theta_r \implies \mu_s = \frac{\sin\theta_r}{\cos\theta_r} = \tan\theta_r$$

Thus, the coefficient of static friction $\mu_s$ equals the tangent of the angle of repose $\theta_r$.

2. Kinetic Friction & Incline Acceleration

When the inclination angle exceeds the angle of repose ($\theta > \theta_r$), the net driving force accelerates the slider down the plane:

$$F_{\text{net}} = mg \sin\theta - f_k = mg \sin\theta - \mu_k mg \cos\theta = ma$$ $$a = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k \cos\theta)$$

3. Work-Energy Theorem & Thermal Dissipation Accounting

According to the Work-Energy Theorem, the net work done by all forces on the block equals its change in kinetic energy:

$$W_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{gravity}} + W_{\text{friction}} = \Delta K$$ $$W_{\text{gravity}} = mg \cdot \Delta h = mgs \sin\theta = -\Delta U$$ $$W_{\text{friction}} = -f_k \cdot s = -(\mu_k mg \cos\theta) \cdot s$$

Rearranging the energy conservation identity:

$$-\Delta U - (f_k \cdot s) = K_f \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad -\Delta U = K_f + W_{\text{friction}}$$

This proves that the gravitational potential energy lost by the slider ($-\Delta U$) is converted into kinetic energy ($K_f$) plus the thermal energy dissipated by kinetic friction ($W_{\text{friction}}$).

4. Photogate Speed Measurement Principle

When the slider of finite length $L_{\text{block}}$ passes through a photogate at position $s$, the infrared beam is interrupted for a transit duration $\Delta t_{\text{gate}}$. The instantaneous terminal velocity is quantified as:

$$v_{\text{measured}} = \frac{L_{\text{block}}}{\Delta t_{\text{gate}}}$$ $$K_{\text{exp}} = \frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{measured}}^2$$

📚 Comprehensive Experimental Manual & Lab Guidelines

1. Objective & Physical Principles

To determine the coefficient of static friction between different material pairs on an inclined plane, identify the angle of repose $\mu_s = \tan\theta_r$, and verify the Work-Energy Theorem by demonstrating that the loss of potential energy $-\Delta U = mg\Delta h$ equals the sum of terminal kinetic energy $K_f = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2$ and dissipated friction work $W_f = f_k \cdot s$.

2. Step-by-Step Experimental Procedure
  1. Select the friction surface pair (e.g. Hardwood on Polished Wood). Note $\mu_s = 0.40, \mu_k = 0.30$.
  2. Adjust the ramp angle $\theta$ above the angle of repose $\theta_r = \arctan(\mu_s)$ so that positive acceleration occurs.
  3. Set the desired slide travel distance $s$ (e.g. $0.80\text{ m}$) and block mass $m$ (e.g. $0.20\text{ kg}$).
  4. Click Release Block to start the dynamic simulation. Watch the real-time stacked energy bar transfer potential energy into kinetic and thermal work.
  5. When the block interrupts Gate B, motion halts, and the Energy Ledger records $-\Delta U, K_f, W_f$, verifying $-\Delta U = K_f + W_f$ with zero residual.
  6. Click Log Trial Data to record the trial into the observation table and repeat for different angles and distances.
3. Mathematical Formulas & Telemetry Legend
  • Vertical Drop: $\Delta h = s \sin\theta$
  • Potential Energy Loss: $-\Delta U = mg\Delta h = mgs\sin\theta$
  • Kinetic Friction Force: $f_k = \mu_k N = \mu_k mg \cos\theta$
  • Thermal Work Dissipated: $W_f = f_k \cdot s$
  • Terminal Velocity: $v_f = \sqrt{2as} = \sqrt{2gs(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta)}$
  • Terminal Kinetic Energy: $K_f = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2$
  • Energy Balance Residual: $\epsilon = |-\Delta U - (K_f + W_f)| = 0.000\text{ J}$
