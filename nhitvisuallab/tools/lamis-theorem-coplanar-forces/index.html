📖 Mechanical Principles & Mathematical Derivations Syllabus Reference ES102/ES104

1. Statement of Lami’s Theorem

If three concurrent, coplanar forces acting at a common point (the central knot/ring \(O\)) are in static equilibrium, each force is directly proportional to the sine of the angle subtended between the other two forces:

$$\mathbf{\frac{P}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{Q}{\sin\beta} = \frac{R}{\sin\gamma} = \text{Constant } k}$$

Where:

  • \(P, Q, R\): Magnitudes of the three concurrent tensile forces (in Newtons \(\text{N}\) or gram-force \(\text{gf}\)).
  • \(\alpha\): Angle between the lines of action of force \(Q\) and force \(R\).
  • \(\beta\): Angle between the lines of action of force \(P\) and force \(R\).
  • \(\gamma\): Angle between the lines of action of force \(P\) and force \(Q\).
  • Geometric Invariant: \(\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 360^\circ = 2\pi\text{ rad}\).

2. Mathematical Derivation via Triangle Law & Sine Rule

Consider three forces \(\vec{P}, \vec{Q}, \vec{R}\) acting in equilibrium at a point \(O\). By the Triangle Law of Forces, if three concurrent coplanar forces are in equilibrium, they can be represented in magnitude and direction by the three sides of a closed triangle taken in order:

$$\vec{P} + \vec{Q} + \vec{R} = \vec{0}$$

In this closed force triangle, the external angles between the sides correspond to the inter-force angles \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\). Consequently, the interior angles \(A, B, C\) opposite to sides \(P, Q, R\) are:

$$A = 180^\circ - \alpha, \quad B = 180^\circ - \beta, \quad C = 180^\circ - \gamma$$

Applying the Law of Sines to the force triangle:

$$\frac{P}{\sin A} = \frac{Q}{\sin B} = \frac{R}{\sin C}$$ $$\implies \frac{P}{\sin(180^\circ - \alpha)} = \frac{Q}{\sin(180^\circ - \beta)} = \frac{R}{\sin(180^\circ - \gamma)}$$

Since \(\sin(180^\circ - \theta) = \sin\theta\), we arrive directly at Lami's Theorem:

$$\mathbf{\frac{P}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{Q}{\sin\beta} = \frac{R}{\sin\gamma}}$$

3. Orthogonal Coordinate Resolution Method (\(\sum F_x = 0, \sum F_y = 0\))

Let each force \(\vec{F}_i\) act at an absolute polar angle \(\theta_i \in [0^\circ, 360^\circ)\) measured counter-clockwise from the horizontal positive \(x\)-axis:

$$\sum F_x = P \cos\theta_1 + Q \cos\theta_2 + R \cos\theta_3 = 0$$ $$\sum F_y = P \sin\theta_1 + Q \sin\theta_2 + R \sin\theta_3 = 0$$ $$R_{\text{res}} = \sqrt{(\sum F_x)^2 + (\sum F_y)^2} \equiv 0$$

4. Triangle Inequality Existence Condition

For a static equilibrium point to exist within the interior of the board, the three forces must be able to form a closed triangle. This requires that the sum of any two forces must strictly exceed the third:

$$P + Q > R, \quad Q + R > P, \quad P + R > Q$$

If \(R \ge P + Q\), the vertical force \(R\) exceeds the maximum possible upward support from cords 1 and 2. The knot accelerates downwards out of bounds, and no physical equilibrium position exists.

📚 Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) & Laboratory Manual WBSCTE ES102 Lab
1. Apparatus Description & Objective ▾
Apparatus: Gravesande's Apparatus (Vertical Force Table), two frictionless clamped pulleys, three slotted weight hangers with calibrated disc masses, central brass ring/knot, sheet of white drawing paper, drawing pins, virtual planar mirror strip, and \(360^\circ\) circular protractor.
Objective: To experimentally verify Lami's Theorem and the Triangle Law of Forces for three coplanar, concurrent forces in static equilibrium.
2. Step-by-Step Experimental Procedure ▾
  1. Secure a clean sheet of white drawing paper onto the vertical wooden board using corner drawing pins.
  2. Position the left and right pulleys along the top frame rail at desired angles (e.g., \(140^\circ\) and \(40^\circ\)).
  3. Load slotted weights onto the hangers: set \(P = 50\text{ gf}\), \(Q = 60\text{ gf}\), and \(R = 70\text{ gf}\).
  4. Verify that the triangle inequality \(P + Q > R\) is satisfied before releasing the knot.
  5. Gently displace the central ring and allow it to settle into its natural equilibrium position \(O\).
  6. Tap the wooden board lightly to eliminate any minor axle friction or cord stiction.
  7. Place the virtual mirror strip behind each cord. Align the cord with its image to mark two parallax-free pin dots on the paper.
  8. Remove cords and project the lines of action through knot \(O\). Measure angles \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) using the protractor.
  9. Calculate \(\frac{P}{\sin\alpha}, \frac{Q}{\sin\beta}, \frac{R}{\sin\gamma}\) and verify equality within experimental error (< 1.5%).
3. Parallax Elimination & Mirror Strip Technique ▾
When sighting cords on a vertical drawing board, line-of-sight misalignment introduces severe parallax error. By positioning a flat plane mirror strip flat behind the cord, the student views the cord with one eye closed and moves until the physical cord exactly covers its virtual reflection. Marking two points along this line ensures that the pencil dots lie exactly on the true line of action of the tensile force.
4. Precautions & Sources of Experimental Error ▾
  • Pulleys must be clean, frictionless, and lubricated to minimize cord drag.
  • The central ring should be lightweight so its own gravitational weight is negligible compared to suspended loads.
  • Cords must not rub against the drawing paper or board frame.
  • Tapping the board gently before taking readings eliminates microscopic pulley bearing stiction.
