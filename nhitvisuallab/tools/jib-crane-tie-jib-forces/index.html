📖 Mechanical Principles & Mathematical Derivations Syllabus Reference ES102/ES104

1. Structural Frame Geometry & Triangle Law

A wall-mounted simple jib crane comprises a triangular pin-jointed truss mounted to a rigid vertical wall: an upper wall pivot \(A(0, h)\), a lower wall pivot \(B(0, 0)\), and an apex crane head joint \(C(x_C, y_C)\). The upper member \(AC\) is the Tie Rod (subjected to tensile pull \(T\)), while the lower member \(BC\) is the Jib Boom (subjected to compressive thrust \(C\)).

$$\cos\theta_C = \frac{L_{\text{tie}}^2 + L_{\text{jib}}^2 - h^2}{2 \cdot L_{\text{tie}} \cdot L_{\text{jib}}}$$ $$\cos\theta_{\text{jib}} = \frac{h^2 + L_{\text{jib}}^2 - L_{\text{tie}}^2}{2 \cdot h \cdot L_{\text{jib}}}, \quad \beta = 90^\circ - \theta_{\text{jib}}$$ $$\cos\theta_{\text{tie}} = \frac{h^2 + L_{\text{tie}}^2 - L_{\text{jib}}^2}{2 \cdot h \cdot L_{\text{tie}}}, \quad \alpha = \theta_{\text{tie}} - 90^\circ$$ $$\theta_C = \alpha + \beta$$

2. Static Equilibrium of Apex Joint \(C\) (Method of Joints)

At the apex joint \(C\), three concurrent, coplanar forces act in static equilibrium: the downward suspended load \(\vec{W}\), the tensile pull \(\vec{T}\) of the tie rod acting at inclination \(\alpha\) above the horizontal, and the compressive reaction thrust \(\vec{C}\) of the jib strut pushing outward at inclination \(\beta\) above the horizontal.

$$\sum F_x = -T \cos\alpha + C \cos\beta = 0 \implies \mathbf{T \cos\alpha = C \cos\beta}$$ $$\sum F_y = T \sin\alpha + C \sin\beta - W = 0 \implies \mathbf{T \sin\alpha + C \sin\beta = W}$$

Solving these orthogonal equilibrium equations simultaneously yields the closed-form solutions:

$$\mathbf{T = W \cdot \frac{\cos\beta}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} = W \cdot \frac{\cos\beta}{\sin\theta_C}}$$ $$\mathbf{C = W \cdot \frac{\cos\alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} = W \cdot \frac{\cos\alpha}{\sin\theta_C}}$$

3. Lami's Theorem Formulation

By Lami's Theorem for three concurrent forces in static equilibrium, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle subtended between the other two forces:

$$\frac{W}{\sin\theta_C} = \frac{T}{\sin(90^\circ + \beta)} = \frac{C}{\sin(90^\circ + \alpha)}$$ $$\implies \mathbf{\frac{W}{\sin\theta_C} = \frac{T}{\cos\beta} = \frac{C}{\cos\alpha} = \text{Constant } k}$$

4. Member Self-Weight Deadweight Correction

In physical practice, the tie rod and tubular jib have finite mass. For uniformly distributed member mass, half of the deadweight of each member is transferred directly to the apex joint \(C\):

$$W_{\text{eff}} = W + \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{tie}} g + \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{jib}} g$$

📚 Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) & Laboratory Manual WBSCTE ES102 Lab
1. Apparatus Overview & Objective ▾
Apparatus: Wall-mounted simple jib crane apparatus consisting of a rigid vertical steel column with adjustable pinned brackets, a tubular steel tie rod with an inline tension spring balance, a tubular steel jib strut with an internal compression spring balance, apex hinge pin with load hook, and a set of cast-iron slotted disc weights.
Objective: To experimentally determine the forces in the tie rod and jib strut of a wall-mounted jib crane for different hook loads, verify the results using the Method of Joints and Lami's Theorem, and construct the closed vector triangle of forces.
2. Step-by-Step Experimental Procedure ▾
  1. Set the vertical bracket spacing \(h = 60.0\text{ cm}\), jib strut length \(L_{\text{jib}} = 75.0\text{ cm}\), and tie rod length \(L_{\text{tie}} = 65.0\text{ cm}\).
  2. Inspect the initial zero readings on both spring balances. Turn the tare adjustment nut to zero out member deadweights.
  3. Suspend an initial hook load of \(W = 50.0\text{ N}\) on apex pin \(C\).
  4. Read the tensile force on the tie rod balance (\(T_{\text{obs}}\)) and the compressive thrust on the jib strut balance (\(C_{\text{obs}}\)).
  5. Calculate the theoretical member forces using the Method of Joints: \(T_{\text{calc}} = W \frac{\cos\beta}{\sin\theta_C}\) and \(C_{\text{calc}} = W \frac{\cos\alpha}{\sin\theta_C}\).
  6. Compute the percentage discrepancy: \(\Delta\% = \frac{|\text{Observed} - \text{Calculated}|}{\text{Calculated}} \times 100\%\).
  7. Increment the load in steps of \(50\text{ N}\) up to \(250\text{ N}\). Record all trials in the laboratory ledger.
  8. Construct the closed force triangle for each trial and confirm that the polygon closes with negligible closure error.
3. Nature of Member Forces: Tie vs. Jib ▾
Tie Rod (\(AC\)): The upper member is subjected to Tension. The downward load pulls apex \(C\) clockwise away from upper bracket \(A\), stretching the tubular spring inside the balance.
Jib Boom (\(BC\)): The lower member is subjected to Compression (Thrust). Apex \(C\) pushes inward towards wall bracket \(B\), compressing the internal coil spring inside the sleeve.
4. Precautions & Sources of Error ▾
  • The apex pin must be free to rotate without binding or friction stiction.
  • Spring balances must be tared before applying the hook load to prevent member deadweight bias.
  • The triangle inequality condition \(L_{\text{tie}} + h > L_{\text{jib}}\) must be strictly satisfied for frame stability.
  • Load should be suspended gently to avoid dynamic impact loading on spring balances.
