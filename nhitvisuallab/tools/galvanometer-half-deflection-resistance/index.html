📚 Laboratory Practical Manual & Standard Operating Procedures

1. Theoretical Foundation of the Half-Deflection Method

A pointer-type moving-coil galvanometer consists of a light rectangular coil suspended in a radial magnetic field produced by concave cylindrical pole pieces and a soft-iron cylindrical core.

When a current \(I_g\) passes through the coil of \(N\) turns and area \(A\), the deflecting magnetic torque is \(\tau_m = N I_g A B\). This is balanced by the restoring torque of phosphor-bronze hairsprings \(\tau_r = C \theta\), establishing direct proportionality: \[I_g = k \cdot \theta, \quad \text{where } k = \frac{C}{NAB}\]

Half-Deflection Principle:
1. With \(K_1\) closed and \(K_2\) open: \(I_g = \frac{E}{R + G} = k\theta\).
2. When shunt \(S\) is connected by closing \(K_2\), the total circuit resistance decreases to \(R + \frac{GS}{G+S}\). The current branching through \(G\) is: \[I_g' = \frac{E \cdot S}{R(G + S) + GS}\] When \(S\) is adjusted such that \(\theta' = \theta/2\), \(I_g' = I_g/2\), yielding: \[\mathbf{G = \frac{R \cdot S}{R - S}}\]

2. Step-by-Step Practical Half-Deflection Procedure
  1. Inspect the galvanometer; verify the pointer indicates exactly zero divisions. Adjust the mechanical zero knob if needed.
  2. Connect the DC accumulator, high resistance box \(R\), plug key \(K_1\), galvanometer \(G\), shunt box \(S\), and plug key \(K_2\) according to the standard wiring schematic.
  3. Ensure keys \(K_1\) and \(K_2\) are open. Unplug a high resistance from \(R\) (e.g., \(5000\ \Omega\)).
  4. Insert key \(K_1\) (keeping \(K_2\) open). Adjust \(R\) until the needle deflects to an even number of divisions near full scale (\(\theta = 30, 28, 26\dots\)). Note \(R\) and \(\theta\).
  5. Unplug an estimated resistance from shunt box \(S\) (e.g., \(80\ \Omega\)). Insert key \(K_2\).
  6. Fine-tune shunt resistance \(S\) until the needle settles at exactly \(\theta/2\) divisions. Note \(S\).
  7. Compute \(G = \frac{RS}{R - S}\) and \(k = \frac{E}{(R+G)\theta}\).
  8. Repeat for 4–5 distinct initial even deflections to compute mean values.
3. Conversion into a Multi-Range Ammeter & Wire Sizing

To convert the galvanometer (\(G, I_g^{\text{max}}\)) to measure higher currents up to \(I\), a low-resistance shunt \(S\) is wired in parallel: \[S = \frac{I_g \cdot G}{I - I_g}\]

Shunt Wire Sizing: Using standard resistance alloy wire of resistivity \(\rho\) (e.g., Manganin, \(\rho = 48.2 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}\)) and diameter \(D\): \[S = \rho \frac{L}{A} = \frac{4 \rho L}{\pi D^2} \implies \mathbf{L = \frac{S \pi D^2}{4 \rho}}\] The measured wire length is cut, soldered across the galvanometer binding posts, and tested against a standard reference ammeter.

4. Conversion into a Multi-Range Voltmeter & Multiplier Resistor

To measure voltage up to \(V\), a large multiplier resistance \(R_{\text{series}}\) is connected in series with the galvanometer: \[V = I_g(G + R_{\text{series}}) \implies \mathbf{R_{\text{series}} = \frac{V}{I_g} - G}\] The converted voltmeter is calibrated against a standard master voltmeter across 10 equidistant potential steps using a potential divider circuit.

Technical Deep-Dive: Ballistic Dynamics, Damping & Meter Conversion Principles

The moving-coil (D'Arsonval) galvanometer is the foundational electromechanical instrument used throughout classical electrical metrology. In addition to high sensitivity, its mathematical predictability enables seamless transformation into both current-measuring ammeters and voltage-measuring voltmeters.

1. Second-Order Torsional Oscillator Dynamics

The mechanical motion of the suspended coil with moment of inertia \(J\), spring constant \(C\), and damping coefficient \(b\) is governed by: \[J \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} + b \frac{d\theta}{dt} + C \theta = N A B \cdot i_g(t)\] The damping term \(b\) has two components: air friction (\(b_{\text{air}}\)) and electromagnetic back-EMF damping: \[b_{\text{em}} = \frac{N^2 A^2 B^2}{R_{\text{total}}}\] When shunt key \(K_2\) is open, \(R_{\text{total}} \approx R + G \approx 5000\ \Omega\), causing \(b_{\text{em}}\) to be small and producing light underdamped oscillations. When \(K_2\) closes, the external loop resistance drops to \(S \approx 80\ \Omega\), dramatically increasing \(b_{\text{em}}\) and inducing rapid critical damping.

2. Calibration & Error Analysis

During calibration against a master standard meter, the absolute deviation \(\Delta = M_{\text{converted}} - M_{\text{standard}}\) is plotted across the full range. High-grade conversions maintain error \(\le \pm 1.5\%\), complying with international laboratory tolerance standards.

📖 Galvanometer Half-Deflection Mathematical Derivations

1. Half-Deflection Condition

\(I_g = \frac{E}{R + G} = k\theta\) (with K2 open)
\(I_g' = \frac{E \cdot S}{R(G + S) + GS} = \frac{I_g}{2} = k \frac{\theta}{2}\) (with K2 closed)
\(\implies \mathbf{G = \frac{R \cdot S}{R - S}}\)

2. Ammeter Shunt & Wire Sizing
