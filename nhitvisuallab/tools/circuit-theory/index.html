Circuit Theory & Networks Theoretical Formulations

1. Modified Nodal Analysis (MNA) — Systematic Circuit Solution

MNA combines Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) with Ohm's Law to solve circuits with $n$ nodes and $b$ branches. With node voltages $\mathbf{v}$ as unknowns:

$$\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{i}_s$$

where the conductance matrix $\mathbf{G}$ has diagonal entries $G_{kk} = \sum_j g_{kj}$ (sum of conductances incident to node $k$) and off-diagonal entries $G_{kj} = -g_{kj}$ (negative conductance between nodes $k$ and $j$). KCL at each node $k$:

$$\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(k)} \frac{v_k - v_j}{R_{kj}} = I_{s,k}$$

Voltage sources are introduced as branches with current unknowns, extending $\mathbf{G}$ to a stamp matrix. The solution $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{G}^{-1}\,\mathbf{i}_s$ yields all node potentials and branch currents.

2. Thevenin & Norton Equivalent Theorems

Thevenin's Theorem: Any linear, bilateral two-terminal network can be replaced by a single ideal voltage source $V_{th}$ in series with a single resistance $R_{th}$:

$$V_{th} = V_{oc} \quad \text{(Open-circuit voltage at terminals A-B)}$$

$$R_{th} = \frac{V_{oc}}{I_{sc}} = R_{eq}\big|_{\text{all independent sources killed}}$$

Norton's Theorem: Equivalent to Thevenin but with current source $I_N$ in parallel with $R_{th}$:

$$I_N = I_{sc} = \frac{V_{th}}{R_{th}}$$

Load current: $I_L = \dfrac{V_{th}}{R_{th} + R_L}$,   Load voltage: $V_L = V_{th} \cdot \dfrac{R_L}{R_{th} + R_L}$

3. Maximum Power Transfer (MPT) Theorem

Power delivered to load resistance $R_L$ from a Thevenin equivalent is:

$$P_L(R_L) = I_L^2\,R_L = \left(\frac{V_{th}}{R_{th}+R_L}\right)^2 R_L$$

Differentiating $\frac{dP_L}{dR_L} = 0$ yields the condition for maximum power transfer:

$$\boxed{R_L = R_{th}} \implies P_{max} = \frac{V_{th}^2}{4\,R_{th}}$$

At MPT, source efficiency $\eta = \dfrac{P_L}{P_{source}} = 50\%$. For AC circuits, maximum power transfer requires $Z_L = Z_{th}^*$ (complex conjugate matching):

$$R_L = R_{th}, \quad X_L = -X_{th}$$

4. R-L-C Series AC Resonance

In a series RLC circuit driven by $v_s = V_m\sin(\omega t)$, impedance is:

$$Z(\omega) = R + j\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right) = R + j\,X_{net}$$

Resonant frequency (when $X_L = X_C$, $\angle Z = 0$):

$$f_r = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} \,\text{Hz}, \qquad \omega_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \,\text{rad/s}$$

Quality Factor (sharpness of resonance):

$$Q = \frac{\omega_r L}{R} = \frac{1}{\omega_r C R} = \frac{1}{R}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}$$

Bandwidth and half-power (−3 dB) frequencies:

$$BW = f_2 - f_1 = \frac{f_r}{Q} = \frac{R}{2\pi L}$$

$$f_{1,2} = f_r\left(\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4Q^2}} \mp \frac{1}{2Q}\right)$$

At resonance, $|Z|_{min} = R$, current is maximum $I_{max} = V_s/R$, and $V_L = V_C = Q\cdot V_s$ (voltage magnification).

Vth — Open-Circuit Terminal Voltage

The Thevenin voltage is the open-circuit voltage appearing across terminals A-B with the load disconnected. It equals the Superposition of contributions from all independent sources in the network. With R2 shunting the mid-node: $V_{th} = V_s \cdot \dfrac{R_2 \| R_3}{R_1 + R_2 \| R_3}$ (simplified 3-resistor case).

Measurement Method:Voltmeter across A-B, RL open
Theorem Source:L. C. Thévenin, 1883

Peak Point — RL = Rth

Maximum power is delivered when load resistance equals Thevenin resistance. At this conjugate-matched condition, $P_{max} = V_{th}^2/(4R_{th})$ and exactly half the total generated power is consumed by the load, the other half dissipated internally. L — Inductor (Inductive Reactance XL)

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field. Its inductive reactance $X_L = 2\pi f L\,\Omega$ increases linearly with frequency. Voltage across inductor leads current by exactly 90°. At resonance, $X_L = X_C$ so they cancel in the impedance equation, leaving only resistance $R$. This produces minimum impedance and maximum current in the series circuit.

Reactance Formula:XL = 2πfL Ω
Phase Shift:V leads I by 90°
