⚡ 1. PMMC D'Arsonval Principle & Torque Equations

The Permanent Magnet Moving Coil (PMMC) instrument operates on the D'Arsonval principle: when an electric current $I$ passes through a rectangular coil placed within a permanent radial magnetic field $B$, a mechanical deflecting torque $T_d$ is produced:

$$T_d = N \cdot B \cdot I \cdot l \cdot b = N B A I = G \cdot I$$

Where $N$ is turns, $A = l \cdot b$ is coil area, and $G = NBA$ is the displacement constant ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m/A}$).

Controlling torque is provided by twin phosphor-bronze hairsprings coiled in opposite directions to cancel temperature drift:

$$T_c = K \cdot \theta$$

At steady state balance ($T_d = T_c$), we obtain the fundamental linear scale law:

$$\theta_{\text{ss}} = \left(\frac{G}{K}\right) I \implies \mathbf{\theta \propto I} \quad (\text{Uniform Linear Scale})$$

🔌 2. Ammeter Shunts ($R_{\text{sh}}$) & Voltmeter Multipliers ($R_{\text{se}}$)

Standard basic meter movement has full-scale current $I_m$ and internal coil resistance $R_m$ (full-scale voltage drop $V_m = I_m R_m$).

  • DC Ammeter Extension (Range $I = m \cdot I_m$): A low-resistance shunt resistor $R_{\text{sh}}$ made of Manganin (negligible temperature coefficient) is connected in parallel with the coil:
    $$R_{\text{sh}} = \frac{I_m R_m}{I - I_m} = \frac{R_m}{m - 1}$$
  • DC Voltmeter Extension (Range $V = m \cdot V_m$): A high-resistance multiplier resistor $R_{\text{se}}$ is connected in series with the coil:
    $$R_{\text{se}} = \frac{V - V_m}{I_m} = \frac{V}{I_m} - R_m = R_m(m - 1)$$
  • Sensitivity ($S$): Ohms-per-volt rating $S = \frac{1}{I_m} = \frac{R_m + R_{\text{se}}}{V}$. Higher sensitivity minimizes loading burden on measured circuits.

📊 3. Three-Phase Power Measurement (Two-Wattmeter Method)

In a 3-phase 3-wire system (Star or Delta), total active power $P$ and reactive power $Q$ can be measured using two electrodynamometer wattmeters ($W_1, W_2$):

$$W_1 = V_L I_L \cos(30^\circ - \phi), \qquad W_2 = V_L I_L \cos(30^\circ + \phi)$$

Sum of readings yields total active 3-phase power:

$$W_1 + W_2 = V_L I_L [\cos(30^\circ - \phi) + \cos(30^\circ + \phi)] = \mathbf{\sqrt{3} V_L I_L \cos\phi = P_{\text{total}}}$$

Difference of readings yields total reactive 3-phase power:

$$W_1 - W_2 = V_L I_L [\cos(30^\circ - \phi) - \cos(30^\circ + \phi)] = V_L I_L \sin\phi$$ $$Q_{\text{total}} = \sqrt{3}(W_1 - W_2) = \sqrt{3} V_L I_L \sin\phi$$

Power factor angle $\phi$ is determined directly without knowing $V_L$ or $I_L$:

$$\tan\phi = \sqrt{3}\left(\frac{W_1 - W_2}{W_1 + W_2}\right) \implies \text{PF} = \cos\phi = \cos\left[\arctan\left(\sqrt{3}\frac{W_1 - W_2}{W_1 + W_2}\right)\right]$$

⚡ 4. Four Canonical Power Factor Regimes & Reversal Switch

The behavior of $W_1$ and $W_2$ depends directly on load phase angle $\phi$:

  • $\phi = 0^\circ$ ($\text{PF} = 1.0$, Pure Resistive): $W_1 = W_2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} V_L I_L$. Both wattmeters give identical positive readings.
  • $\phi = 30^\circ$ ($\text{PF} = 0.866\text{ lag}$): $W_1 = V_L I_L \cos(0^\circ) = V_L I_L$; $W_2 = V_L I_L \cos(60^\circ) = 0.5 V_L I_L$. $W_1 = 2 W_2$.
  • $\phi = 60^\circ$ ($\text{PF} = 0.500\text{ lag}$): $W_1 = V_L I_L \cos(-30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} V_L I_L = P_{\text{total}}$; $W_2 = V_L I_L \cos(90^\circ) = \mathbf{0}$. $W_2$ reads zero.
  • $\phi > 60^\circ$ ($\text{PF} < 0.500\text{ lag}$): $\cos(30^\circ + \phi) < 0 \implies \mathbf{W_2 < 0}$. The meter attempts to deflect backward against the zero pin! The potential coil reversal switch must be flipped to take the upscale reading $|W_2|$, and registered as a negative value: $P = W_1 - |W_2|$.

⚖ 5. Maxwell's Inductance-Capacitance AC Bridge

Maxwell's bridge measures unknown self-inductance $L_x$ and series resistance $R_x$ by comparison with a standard variable capacitor $C_4$:

$$Z_1 = R_x + j\omega L_x, \quad Z_2 = R_2, \quad Z_3 = R_3, \quad Z_4 = \frac{R_4}{1 + j\omega C_4 R_4}$$

At null balance condition ($Z_1 Z_4 = Z_2 Z_3$):

$$(R_x + j\omega L_x)\left(\frac{R_4}{1 + j\omega C_4 R_4}\right) = R_2 R_3 \implies R_x R_4 + j\omega L_x R_4 = R_2 R_3 + j\omega C_4 R_2 R_3 R_4$$

Equating real and imaginary components yields two completely independent (orthogonal) equations:

$$\mathbf{R_x = \frac{R_2 R_3}{R_4}}, \qquad \mathbf{L_x = R_2 R_3 C_4}$$

Notice that the excitation frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$ cancels out entirely, proving that balance is frequency-independent!

🎪 6. Coil Quality Factor ($Q$) & Bridge Scope

The Quality Factor $Q$ represents the ratio of inductive reactance to series resistance:

$$Q = \frac{\omega L_x}{R_x} = \frac{\omega (R_2 R_3 C_4)}{(R_2 R_3 / R_4)} = \mathbf{\omega C_4 R_4 = 2\pi f C_4 R_4}$$
  • Medium-Q Coils ($1 < Q < 10$): Ideal operating regime for Maxwell's bridge. Both $R_4$ and $C_4$ balance dials operate in practical, stable decades.
  • High-Q Coils ($Q > 10$): Require an excessively large resistor $R_4$ ($> 100\text{ k}\Omega$), making commercial resistors impractical. Hay's Bridge (series $R_4-C_4$ arm) is used instead.
  • Low-Q Coils ($Q < 1$): Suffer from sliding balance and convergence difficulties. Anderson's Bridge is preferred.
