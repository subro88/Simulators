Curriculum Theory, Mathematical Proofs & Derivations

1. Induction Motor Thevenin Equivalent Reduction

Converting the stator impedance and magnetizing branch into a single Thevenin equivalent voltage source and impedance seen from the rotor terminals:

$$V_{\text{th}} = V_{\text{ph}} \frac{X_m}{\sqrt{R_1^2 + (X_1 + X_m)^2}}$$
$$Z_{\text{th}} = R_{\text{th}} + j X_{\text{th}} = \frac{j X_m (R_1 + j X_1)}{R_1 + j(X_1 + X_m)}$$

Using standard lab values ($V_{\text{ph}} = 239.6\text{ V}, R_1 = 0.5\ \Omega, X_1 = 1.2\ \Omega, X_m = 35\ \Omega$), $V_{\text{th}} \approx 231.64\text{ V}$, $R_{\text{th}} \approx 0.467\ \Omega$, $X_{\text{th}} \approx 1.185\ \Omega$.

2. Maximum Breakdown Torque & Pull-Out Slip

Applying the Maximum Power Transfer Theorem to rotor power transferred across the air-gap gives the breakdown slip and breakdown torque:

$$s_m = \frac{R_2' + R_{\text{ext}}'}{\sqrt{R_{\text{th}}^2 + (X_{\text{th}} + X_2')^2}}$$
$$T_{\max} = \frac{3 V_{\text{th}}^2}{2 \omega_s \left[ R_{\text{th}} + \sqrt{R_{\text{th}}^2 + (X_{\text{th}} + X_2')^2} \right]}$$

Crucial Insight: $T_{\max}$ is completely independent of rotor resistance $R_2'$. Adding external resistance $R_{\text{ext}}'$ shifts the slip at maximum torque $s_m$ towards standstill ($s_m = 1.0$), dramatically improving starting torque without reducing maximum breakdown capability!

3. Starting Switchgear: Inrush Current & Torque Reduction

Comparing Direct-On-Line (DOL), Star-Delta ($Y$-$\Delta$), and Autotransformer starting:

$$\frac{I_{\text{st, } Y\text{-}\Delta}}{I_{\text{st, DOL}}} = \frac{1}{3}, \quad \frac{T_{\text{st, } Y\text{-}\Delta}}{T_{\text{st, DOL}}} = \frac{1}{3}$$
$$\frac{I_{\text{st, auto}}}{I_{\text{st, DOL}}} = x^2, \quad \frac{T_{\text{st, auto}}}{T_{\text{st, DOL}}} = x^2$$

Where $x$ is the autotransformer tapping ratio ($0.50, 0.65, 0.80$). At a 65% tap ($x=0.65$), starting current and starting torque are reduced to $x^2 = 42.25\%$ of full DOL inrush.

4. Synchronous Machine V-Curves & Inverted V-Curves

For a cylindrical-rotor machine connected to an infinite bus ($V_t = 240\text{ V/ph}$, $X_s = 4.0\ \Omega$):

$$P = 3 \frac{V_t E_f}{X_s} \sin\delta \implies \sin\delta = \frac{P X_s}{3 V_t E_f}$$
$$\vec{I}_a = \frac{\vec{V}_t - \vec{E}_f}{j X_s} \implies I_a = \frac{\sqrt{V_t^2 + E_f^2 - 2 V_t E_f \cos\delta}}{X_s}$$

At Normal Excitation ($E_f \cos\delta = V_t$), the power factor is unity ($\cos\phi = 1.00$) and armature current is minimum ($I_a = I_{a,\min}$). Over-excitation causes the machine to supply leading reactive power (Synchronous Condenser operation).

5. Parallel Synchronization: Two-Bright One-Dark Method

Lamps connected across the incoming alternator and live bus: Lamp 1 across Phase $R_1$-$R_2$, Lamp 2 across $Y_1$-$B_2$, Lamp 3 across $B_1$-$Y_2$:

$$V_{L1} = 2 V \sin\left(\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}\right), \quad V_{L2, L3} = 2 V \sin\left(\frac{\Delta\theta \pm 120^\circ}{2}\right)$$

At exact synchronism ($\Delta\theta = 0^\circ$ and $\Delta f = 0$), Lamp 1 is completely dark ($V_{L1} = 0$), while Lamps 2 and 3 glow with equal, maximum brilliance. The synchroscope needle points dead vertically (12 o'clock).

6. Ferraris Theorem: Constant Rotating Magnetic Field

Three spatial coils $120^\circ$ apart energized by three-phase balanced sinusoidal currents:

$$\Phi_R = \Phi_m \cos(\omega t), \quad \Phi_Y = \Phi_m \cos(\omega t - 120^\circ), \quad \Phi_B = \Phi_m \cos(\omega t - 240^\circ)$$
$$\vec{\Phi}_r = \Phi_R + \Phi_Y e^{j 120^\circ} + \Phi_B e^{j 240^\circ} = \frac{3}{2} \Phi_m \angle (\omega t)$$

The vector sum produces a constant spatial amplitude of $\frac{3}{2}\Phi_m$ rotating smoothly at synchronous speed $N_s = 120 f / P$.
