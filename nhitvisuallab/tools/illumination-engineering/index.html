Syllabus Coverage — WBSCTE EE/S5/ILE Illumination Engineering

1. Fundamental Laws of Illumination

Inverse Square Law: $E = \frac{I}{d^2}$. Illuminance is inversely proportional to square of distance from point source.
Lambert's Cosine Law: $E = \frac{I \cos \theta}{d^2}$, where $\theta$ is the angle of incidence.

2. Lumen (Light Flux) Method of Design

$N = \frac{E \times A}{F \times UF \times MF}$. Space-to-Height Ratio ($SHR \le 1.5$) ensures uniform illumination without dark scalloping between fittings.

3. Polar Candela Distribution Curves

Rousseau's diagram and Goniophotometer measurement in horizontal and vertical planes to determine Mean Spherical Candle Power ($MSCP$) and Mean Hemispherical Candle Power ($MHCP$).

4. Modern Solid-State LED Lighting

High luminous efficacy ($>120\text{ lm/W}$), Color Rendering Index ($CRI > 80$), Correlated Color Temperature ($CCT = 3000K-6500K$), and Electronic Driver THD ($<10\%$).
