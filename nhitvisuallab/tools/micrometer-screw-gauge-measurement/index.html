WBSCTE Laboratory Manual — Experiment #2

1. Aim of the Experiment

To measure the diameter of a given specimen wire using a Micrometer Screw Gauge, and hence determine its cross-sectional area and volume.

2. Apparatus Required

  • Micrometer Screw Gauge (Range 0–15 mm, Pitch 0.5 mm, Least Count $0.01\text{ mm}$).
  • Specimen wire (Copper, Nichrome, Brass, or Steel).
  • Half-meter ruler (to measure wire length $L$).
  • Analytical balance (to determine wire mass $M$).

3. Principle & Formulae

The screw gauge works on the principle of a micrometer screw: linear advance of the spindle is directly proportional to circular rotation of the thimble.

1. Pitch of the Screw: Linear distance moved by the spindle on the main scale in one complete rotation of the circular head:

$$\text{Pitch} = \frac{\text{Distance moved on Main Scale}}{\text{Number of Complete Rotations}} = \frac{2.5\text{ mm}}{5} = 0.5\text{ mm}$$

2. Least Count ($LC$): The smallest linear displacement measurable:

$$LC = \frac{\text{Pitch}}{\text{Total Circular Scale Divisions (CSD)}} = \frac{0.5\text{ mm}}{50} = 0.01\text{ mm}$$

3. Zero Error ($e$) & Correction:

  • Positive Zero Error ($+e$): When circular scale $0$ is below the datum line when jaws close $\implies e = +(n \times LC)$. Correction is subtracted.
  • Negative Zero Error ($-e$): When circular scale $0$ is above the datum line $\implies e = -(N - n) \times LC$. Correction is added.
$$D_{\text{obs}} = \text{MSR} + (\text{CSR} \times LC)$$ $$D_{\text{true}} = D_{\text{obs}} - (\pm e)$$

4. Cross-Sectional Area ($A$) & Volume ($V$):

$$A = \frac{\pi D_{\text{true}}^2}{4}\text{ mm}^2$$ $$V = A \times L = \frac{\pi D_{\text{true}}^2}{4} \times L\text{ mm}^3$$

4. Precautions & Sources of Error

  1. Always advance the screw using the ratchet stop, never by forcibly gripping the thimble, to ensure uniform contact pressure.
  2. Measure the diameter at three distinct positions along the wire and in two mutually perpendicular directions at each position to account for non-uniformity and ovality.
  3. Check for backlash error caused by wear in the screw threads; always advance the thimble in one direction when approaching contact.
