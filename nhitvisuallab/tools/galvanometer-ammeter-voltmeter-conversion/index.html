📖 Mathematical Engine & Governing Equations

A moving coil galvanometer is an extremely sensitive current-detecting instrument. Its coil burns or saturates if subjected to currents higher than full-scale deflection current $I_g$. Conversion into practical ammeters and voltmeters relies on precise resistance division networks.

1. Full-Scale Galvanometer Parameters:
Full-Scale Deflection Current: $$I_g = n \cdot k$$ Full-Scale Coil Voltage Drop: $$V_g = I_g \cdot G$$ where $n = 30\text{ divisions}$, $k$ is figure of merit ($\text{A/div}$), and $G$ is internal coil resistance ($\Omega$).
2. Conversion to Ammeter (Parallel Shunt Resistor $S$):
Because the shunt is connected in parallel with the coil, potential drop across both branches is equal: $$V_{\text{shunt}} = V_{\text{galv}} \implies (I - I_g) \cdot S = I_g \cdot G \implies \mathbf{S = \frac{I_g \cdot G}{I - I_g}}$$ Physical Shunt Wire Length: $$\mathbf{L_{\text{shunt}} = \frac{S \cdot A}{\rho} = \frac{S \cdot \pi D^2}{4 \rho}}$$ where $D$ is wire diameter measured using the micrometer screw gauge and $\rho$ is the alloy resistivity.
3. Conversion to Voltmeter (Series Multiplier Resistor $R_{\text{series}}$):
The multiplier resistor is connected in series with the galvanometer coil to limit total branch current to $I_g$ at max potential $V$: $$V = I_g \cdot (G + R_{\text{series}}) \implies \mathbf{R_{\text{series}} = \frac{V}{I_g} - G}$$ Total Voltmeter Resistance: $$R_V = G + R_{\text{series}} \approx R_{\text{series}}$$

📚 Experimental Manual & Standard Operating Procedure

1. Aim of the Experiment & Apparatus Required

Aim: (a) To convert a given moving-coil galvanometer into a direct-reading ammeter of desired range (0 – 3.0 A) and calibrate it against a standard ammeter. (b) To convert the galvanometer into a direct-reading voltmeter of desired range (0 – 15.0 V) and calibrate it against a standard voltmeter.

Apparatus: Weston galvanometer, DC power supply, sliding wire-wound rheostat (0 – 100 Ω, 5A), standard master ammeter/voltmeter (Grade 0.5), micrometer screw gauge, manganin/constantan resistance wire, decade resistance box, soldering clamp, connecting patch cords.

2. Step-by-Step Procedure for Ammeter Conversion
  1. Note down galvanometer internal resistance $G$ and figure of merit $k$ to obtain $I_g = n \cdot k$.
  2. Calculate the theoretical required shunt resistance $S = \frac{I_g G}{I - I_g}$.
  3. Open the Micrometer Screw Gauge tool to measure the diameter $D$ of the chosen resistance wire at 3 points.
  4. Calculate required length $L_{\text{shunt}} = \frac{S \pi D^2}{4\rho}$. Add 2 cm extra for soldering/terminal clamps.
  5. Cut the wire and solder it across the galvanometer terminal posts. Snap on the 0 – 3 A dial scale.
  6. Connect the converted meter in series with a DC power supply, rheostat, and master standard ammeter.
  7. Vary the rheostat wiper to set needle deflections at $\theta = 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30$ divisions. Record converted and standard ammeter readings, and plot calibration and error curves.
3. Step-by-Step Procedure for Voltmeter Conversion
  1. Calculate required series multiplier resistance $R_{\text{series}} = \frac{V}{I_g} - G$.
  2. Set decade resistance dials to the exact calculated value $R_{\text{series}}$ and connect in series with the galvanometer positive terminal.
  3. Snap on the 0 – 15 V dial scale.
  4. Connect the converted voltmeter in parallel with a standard voltmeter across the variable wiper of the rheostat wired as a potential divider.
  5. Sweep voltage from 0 to 15 V in equal steps of 2.5 V. Note both readings and compute deviations $\Delta V = V_{\text{conv}} - V_{\text{std}}$.
4. Sources of Error & Precautionary Measures
  • Contact Resistance: Ensure all terminal screws and binding posts are firmly tightened with clean brass surfaces.
  • Zero Error: Check and calibrate zero-error on the micrometer screw gauge before measuring wire diameter.
  • Joule Heating: Avoid leaving maximum current passing through the shunt wire continuously to prevent thermal resistivity drift.
  • Parallax Error: Read galvanometer and master meter needles directly from above using the anti-parallax mirror stripe.
