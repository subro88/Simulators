1. Inlet Valve Open (IVO)

Opens 10°–25° Before Top Dead Center (BTDC) to ensure the valve is fully open when the piston begins its downward suction stroke.

2. Inlet Valve Close (IVC)

Closes 30°–50° After Bottom Dead Center (ABDC) to utilize gas momentum for extra air-fuel intake (ram effect).

3. Exhaust Valve Open (EVO)

Opens 30°–50° Before Bottom Dead Center (BBDC) so high pressure exhaust gases escape freely before the upward exhaust stroke.

4. Valve Overlap Angle

The total crankshaft degrees around TDC when BOTH Inlet and Exhaust valves are open simultaneously (typically 20°–40°).

Guided Experiment: Valve Overlap Angle Calculation

1. Set IVO to 20° BTDC.
2. Set EVC to 15° ATDC.
3. Calculate total Valve Overlap = IVO + EVC = 20° + 15° = 35°.
4. Verify the yellow readout badge matches 35°!

📖 User Guide — Valve Timing Diagram Simulator
1 Overview

The Valve Timing Diagram Simulator draws the polar timing diagrams for 4-stroke Petrol (S.I.) and 4-stroke Diesel (C.I.) engines. A valve timing diagram shows, on a circular crank-angle scale, exactly when each valve opens and closes relative to TDC (Top Dead Centre) and BDC (Bottom Dead Centre). It also shows the ignition point for petrol engines or the fuel-injection point for diesel engines.

Use this tool to understand why real engine valves open before TDC/BDC and close after, how valve overlap improves cylinder scavenging, and how adjusting these angles affects engine breathing and power.

2 Adjusting Valve Events

Six sliders control the key timing angles:

  • IVO — Inlet Valve Opens (before TDC, BTDC), typically 10–20° BTDC.
  • IVC — Inlet Valve Closes (after BDC, ABDC), typically 30–60° ABDC.
  • EVO — Exhaust Valve Opens (before BDC, BBDC), typically 40–65° BBDC.
  • EVC — Exhaust Valve Closes (after TDC, ATDC), typically 10–20° ATDC.
  • Ignition Advance (Petrol) or Injection Advance (Diesel) — degrees before TDC when combustion begins.
  • Valve Overlap — automatically computed from IVO + EVC; the period when both valves are open simultaneously near TDC.
3 Reading the Diagram

The outer circle represents 360° of crank rotation. TDC is at the top (0°/360°) and BDC is at the bottom (180°). Coloured arcs show each valve event duration. A highlighted sector marks the Valve Overlap period near TDC between exhaust and intake strokes.

The Inlet Duration = IVO + 180 + IVC. The Exhaust Duration = EVO + 180 + EVC. Longer durations (achieved by opening earlier and closing later) generally increase volumetric efficiency at high RPM at the cost of low-speed torque.

4 S.I. vs C.I. Engines

Petrol (S.I.) engines use a spark plug to ignite a pre-mixed air-fuel charge. Ignition occurs 10–35° BTDC to allow the flame front to fully develop by the time the piston reaches TDC. Diesel (C.I.) engines inject fuel directly into hot compressed air at 15–30° BTDC; combustion starts automatically due to compression heat.

Understanding the 4-Stroke Engine Valve Timing Diagram

A Valve Timing Diagram is a polar (circular) diagram drawn on a 360° crank-angle scale that shows exactly when the inlet and exhaust valves of a 4-stroke engine open and close relative to the piston positions at Top Dead Centre (TDC) and Bottom Dead Centre (BDC). Understanding valve timing is essential for engine design, diagnostics, and tuning.

Why Valves Open Before TDC and BDC

In a theoretically ideal engine, the inlet valve would open exactly at TDC and close exactly at BDC, and the exhaust valve would do the reverse. In reality, valves have mechanical inertia and gas flow takes time to accelerate. Opening the inlet valve a few degrees before TDC (IVO = 10–20° BTDC) ensures the valve is already partially open and air-fuel mixture is beginning to flow when the piston starts the suction stroke. Closing the inlet valve some degrees after BDC (IVC = 30–60° ABDC) exploits the inertia of the incoming charge to continue filling the cylinder even after the piston has begun its upward compression stroke.

Valve Overlap and Scavenging

Valve Overlap is the crank-angle period near TDC (between exhaust and intake strokes) when both the exhaust valve and the inlet valve are simultaneously open. During overlap, the outgoing exhaust gases help draw fresh charge into the cylinder (scavenging). High-performance engines use large overlap angles (40–60°) for better breathing at high RPM, while economy engines use minimal overlap to avoid exhaust gas recirculation at low speeds.

Petrol (S.I.) vs Diesel (C.I.) Timing

In Spark Ignition (S.I.) petrol engines, the spark fires 10–35° BTDC to allow the flame front to develop fully before TDC. In Compression Ignition (C.I.) diesel engines, fuel is injected 15–30° BTDC into highly compressed hot air (compression ratio 16:1 to 22:1), causing autoignition. Diesel engines generally have longer valve durations than petrol engines due to their higher torque requirements at low RPM.

Effect of Timing on Engine Performance

Advancing ignition timing improves efficiency up to the knock limit. Retarding timing reduces power but lowers knock tendency. Late EVO (exhaust valve opening) wastes expansion work; early EVO improves exhaust blowdown but loses expansion energy. Modern variable valve timing (VVT) systems like VTEC and VVT-i adjust IVO and IVC continuously based on engine speed and load, optimising the valve timing diagram dynamically for both low-end torque and high-end power.
