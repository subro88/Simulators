📖 Coordination Chemistry & Hardness Principles

1. Chelate Formation Equilibrium

Disodium EDTA ($\text{Na}_2\text{H}_2\text{Y}$) ionizes in alkaline solution to yield the chelating ligand $\text{H}_2\text{Y}^{2-}$. At $\text{pH} \approx 10$, EDTA forms stable $1:1$ hexadentate complexes with divalent metal cations:

$$\text{Ca}^{2+} + [\text{H}_2\text{Y}]^{2-} \rightleftharpoons [\text{CaY}]^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \quad (\log K_f = 10.7)$$ $$\text{Mg}^{2+} + [\text{H}_2\text{Y}]^{2-} \rightleftharpoons [\text{MgY}]^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \quad (\log K_f = 8.7)$$

2. EBT Metallochromic Indicator Mechanism

Eriochrome Black T (EBT) acts as a metallochromic indicator. Free EBT exists as pure steel-blue $\text{HIn}^{2-}$ at $\text{pH} = 10$. Initial addition of EBT to sample containing $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ forms the wine-red complex $[\text{MgIn}]^-$. Upon adding EDTA titrant, free EDTA displaces EBT from $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ because $K_f([\text{MgY}]^{2-}) \gg K_f([\text{MgIn}]^-)$:

$$[\text{MgIn}]^- \text{ (Wine Red)} + [\text{H}_2\text{Y}]^{2-} \longrightarrow [\text{MgY}]^{2-} + \text{HIn}^{2-} \text{ (Steel Blue)} + \text{H}^+$$

3. Hardness Equations (ppm $\text{CaCO}_3$ Equivalent)

$$\text{Total Hardness (TH)} = \frac{V_1 \times M_{\text{EDTA}} \times 10^5}{V_{\text{sample}}} \quad (\text{ppm as CaCO}_3)$$ $$\text{Permanent Hardness (PH)} = \frac{V_2 \times M_{\text{EDTA}} \times 10^5}{V_{\text{sample}}} \quad (\text{ppm as CaCO}_3)$$ $$\text{Temporary Hardness (TempH)} = \text{TH} - \text{PH} = \frac{(V_1 - V_2) \times M_{\text{EDTA}} \times 10^5}{V_{\text{sample}}}$$

📖 Comprehensive User Manual & Lab Procedure

1. Experimental Principle & Chelation Mechanism

Disodium EDTA ($\text{Na}_2\text{H}_2\text{Y}$) forms stable $1:1$ soluble chelate complexes with $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions in alkaline solution ($\text{pH} = 10.0$). The EBT metallochromic indicator binds $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ to form a wine-red complex. Upon adding EDTA titrant, free EDTA displaces EBT from metal complexes, turning the solution to pure steel blue at the endpoint.

2. Four-Stage Experimental Workflow

Stage 1: Standardize $\approx 0.01\text{ M}$ EDTA against standard hard water ($1.0\text{ mg/mL }\text{CaCO}_3$).

Stage 2: Titrate raw unboiled water sample to find Total Hardness ($V_1$).

Stage 3: Gently boil sample 15 mins to decompose bicarbonates ($\text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2 \to \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow + \text{CO}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}$), filter, and titrate filtrate to find Permanent Hardness ($V_2$).

Stage 4: Compute Temporary Hardness ($\text{TempH} = \text{TH} - \text{PH}$) and WHO drinking water quality rating.

3. Hardness Conversion Factors

• $1\text{ ppm (mg/L)} = 0.10^\circ\text{fH (French Degrees)}$

• $1\text{ ppm (mg/L)} = 0.056^\circ\text{dH (German Degrees)}$

• $1\text{ ppm (mg/L)} = 0.070^\circ\text{Clark (Clark Degrees)}$
