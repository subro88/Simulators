📚 Laboratory Practical Manual & Standard Operating Procedures

1. Principle & Optical Bench Mechanics (Convex Lens)

An optical bench consists of a long, rigid metallic bed (typically \(1.5\text{ m}\) to \(2.0\text{ m}\)) with parallel rails graduated in millimeters. Sliding uprights support the object pin, lens holder, and image pin.

According to the Cartesian Sign Convention with optical center \(O\) as origin: \[\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}\] For a real object placed to the left (\(u = -u_{\text{lab}}\)) forming a real inverted image to the right (\(v = +v_{\text{lab}}\)): \[\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v_{\text{lab}}} - \left(-\frac{1}{u_{\text{lab}}}\right) = \frac{1}{v_{\text{lab}}} + \frac{1}{u_{\text{lab}}} \implies f = \frac{u \cdot v}{u + v}\] To produce a real image, the distance between object pin and image pin must satisfy \(D = u + v \ge 4f\). When \(u = 2f\), \(v = 2f\) and lateral magnification \(m = -1\).

2. Index Correction & Bench Error Determination (\(\Delta u, \Delta v\))

Due to the thickness of the lens and the physical construction of the sliding uprights, the distance read between the index lines of the uprights does not exactly match the distance between the needle tips and the lens pole.

  • Take an index needle of known physical length \(l_0 = 20.00\text{ cm}\).
  • Place one tip against the object pin and the other touching the surface of the lens.
  • Note the bench distance \(u_{\text{obs}} = |x_L - x_O|\).
  • The index error for \(u\) is \(\Delta u = u_{\text{obs}} - l_0\). Corrected distance is \(u = u_{\text{obs}} - \Delta u\).
  • Repeat on the image side to determine \(\Delta v = v_{\text{obs}} - l_0\). Corrected distance is \(v = v_{\text{obs}} - \Delta v\).
3. Tip-to-Tip Parallax Removal Technique

Parallax is the apparent relative movement of two objects when the observer shifts their eye sideways.

  • Place the eye about \(25\text{ cm}\) to \(30\text{ cm}\) away from the image pin and look along the optical axis.
  • Observe the inverted real image of the red object pin formed in the air and the physical blue image pin.
  • Adjust the height of the image pin until both tips are in the same horizontal plane (touching tip-to-tip).
  • Move your head gently from left to right.
  • Rule of Parallax: The object that is farther away appears to move with the eye, while the closer object moves opposite to the eye.
  • Shift the image pin along the bench until both tips remain strictly locked together without relative displacement. Parallax is now completely removed.
4. Concave Lens Focal Length by Auxiliary Convex Lens Method

A concave lens alone cannot form a real image from a real object. To determine its negative focal length:

  1. Use an auxiliary convex lens \(L_1\) to form a real inverted image \(I_1\) of the object pin \(O\) on the bench. Locate \(I_1\) by tip-to-tip parallax removal (\(x_{I1}\)).
  2. Interpose the test concave lens \(L_2\) between \(L_1\) and \(I_1\) at position \(x_{L2}\) (\(x_{L1} < x_{L2} < x_{I1}\)).
  3. The converging beam from \(L_1\) now falls upon \(L_2\); thus \(I_1\) acts as a virtual object for the concave lens: \[u = +(x_{I1} - x_{L2}) > 0\]
  4. Because the concave lens diverges the rays, the image shifts further away to position \(x_{I2}\). Relocate this new real image with the search pin: \[v = +(x_{I2} - x_{L2}) > 0\]
  5. Calculate the focal length: \[\frac{1}{f_2} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{u - v}{u \cdot v} \implies \mathbf{f_2 = \frac{u \cdot v}{u - v}}\] Since \(v > u\), \(u - v < 0\), yielding a strictly negative focal length (\(f_2 < 0\)).
  6. Physical Constraint: For a real image to form, the virtual object distance must satisfy \(u < |f_2|\). If \(u \ge |f_2|\), the emergent rays remain divergent or parallel.

Comprehensive Laboratory Guide: Geometrical Optics on the Optical Bench

The determination of the focal length of spherical thin lenses using an optical bench represents one of the cornerstone experiments in undergraduate and diploma engineering physics (WBSCTE BS104/BS106 Applied Physics-II). The experiment bridges paraxial geometric optics, wave propagation through refracting interfaces, and statistical measurement analysis.

1. Fermat’s Principle and the Gaussian Thin-Lens Approximation

A thin lens has a center thickness that is negligible compared to its radii of curvature (\(R_1, R_2\)). Under paraxial optics—where rays subtend small angles with the optical axis (\(\sin\theta \approx \tan\theta \approx \theta\))—the Lensmaker's Formula gives the refractive power of the lens: \[\frac{1}{f} = (n - 1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right)\] For an equiconvex crown glass lens with \(n = 1.50\) and \(R_1 = -R_2 = 20\text{ cm}\), \(f = \frac{R}{2(n-1)} = \frac{20}{2(0.5)} = 20.0\text{ cm}\).

2. Three Concordant Graphical Focal Length Determinations

To eliminate single-reading experimental bias, three distinct graphical methods are employed:

  • \(u-v\) Hyperbolic Curve: Plotting \(v\) against \(u\) produces a rectangular hyperbola with vertical asymptote \(u = f\) and horizontal asymptote \(v = f\). Drawing the bisector line \(v = u\) from the origin intersects the curve at \((2f, 2f)\), yielding \(f = \frac{u_{\text{intersect}}}{2}\).
  • \(\frac{1}{u}\) vs. \(\frac{1}{v}\) Linear Regression: Plotting \(\frac{1}{v}\) against \(\frac{1}{u}\) yields a straight line with slope \(-1.000\). The intercept on either axis is \(\frac{1}{f}\), allowing direct calculation \(f = \frac{1}{\text{intercept}}\) with correlation coefficient \(R^2 \approx 0.999\).
  • \((u, v)\) Ray Intersection Method: For every observed pair \((u_i, v_i)\), joining \((u_i, 0)\) on the X-axis to \((0, v_i)\) on the Y-axis generates straight lines with equation \(\frac{x}{u_i} + \frac{y}{v_i} = 1\). Since \(\frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f}\), all these lines intersect concurrently at the single point \((f, f)\).

📖 Geometrical Optics Core Equations

1. Thin Lens Gaussian Equation

With optical center as origin and Cartesian signs (\(u < 0, v > 0, f > 0\)):

\(\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} \implies f = \frac{u \cdot v}{u + v}\)

2. Concave Lens Auxiliary Convex Lens Equation

With virtual object distance \(u = +(x_{I1} - x_{L2}) > 0\) and real image distance \(v = +(x_{I2} - x_{L2}) > 0\):

\(\frac{1}{f_2} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{u - v}{u \cdot v} \implies f_2 = \frac{u \cdot v}{u - v} < 0\)

3. Index Error Needle Correction

For calibrated needle of length \(l_0 = 20.00\text{ cm}\):

\(\Delta u = u_{\text{obs}} - l_0, \quad \Delta v = v_{\text{obs}} - l_0\)
\(u_{\text{corr}} = u_{\text{obs}} - \Delta u, \quad v_{\text{corr}} = v_{\text{obs}} - \Delta v\)
