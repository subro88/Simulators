📖 Mathematical Theory & Rheological Formulations

WBSCTE Applied Physics-I (BS103/BS107): Non-Newtonian Yield-Stress Rheology, Entrapment Criteria, and Regularized RK4 Dynamics.

1. Herschel–Bulkley Constitutive Equation

Yield-stress fluids behave as rigid solids when applied shear stress $|\tau| \le \tau_0$, and yield into viscous flow when $|\tau| > \tau_0$:

$$\tau = \tau_0 + K \cdot \dot{\gamma}^n \quad (|\tau| > \tau_0)$$

2. Static Entrapment Criterion & Yield Number ($Y_g$)

The net submerged gravitational driving stress exerted by a sphere of radius $r$ and density $\rho$ in fluid $\sigma$ is:

$$\tau_g = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 (\rho-\sigma)g}{4\pi r^2} = \frac{1}{3} r (\rho - \sigma) g$$ $$Y_g = \frac{\tau_0}{\tau_g} = \frac{\tau_0}{\frac{1}{3} r (\rho - \sigma) g}$$
  • If $Y_g \ge 1 \implies \tau_g \le \tau_0$: Permanent Entrapment ($v=0$). The fluid behaves as an elastic solid matrix.
  • If $Y_g < 1 \implies \tau_g > \tau_0$: Fluid Yields & Sinks. Localized envelope shears and sphere accelerates to $v_t$.

3. Critical Radius Threshold ($r_{\text{crit}}$)

The minimum sphere radius required to overcome yield stress $\tau_0$ and initiate motion is:

$$r_{\text{crit}} = \frac{3 \tau_0}{(\rho - \sigma) g}$$

📚 Comprehensive Experimental Manual & Guidelines

1. Objective & Physical Principles

To investigate Non-Newtonian yield-stress fluids (Toothpaste, Mayonnaise, Drilling Mud, Hair Gel, Ketchup) using Herschel–Bulkley and Bingham Plastic rheological models, quantify static entrapment thresholds ($Y_g \ge 1$), and determine regularized RK4 terminal velocity $v_t$.

2. Step-by-Step Experimental Procedure
  1. Select a Yield-Stress Fluid preset (e.g., Toothpaste, $\tau_0 = 15\text{ Pa}$).
  2. Select a small sphere (e.g. Glass, $r = 1.50\text{ mm}$). Observe that $\tau_g = 5.98\text{ Pa} < \tau_0$, resulting in permanent entrapment ($v=0$).
  3. Switch sphere to Steel ($r = 1.50\text{ mm}$, $\rho = 7850\text{ kg/m}^3$). Observe that $\tau_g = 32.12\text{ Pa} > \tau_0$, fluid yields and sphere sinks.
  4. Click Release Sphere & Start Drop. Observe transit time through Gate A and Gate B and inspect calculated terminal velocity $v_t$.
  5. Log trials into the Observation Table and export CSV for analysis.
