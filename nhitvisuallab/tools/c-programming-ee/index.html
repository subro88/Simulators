🔢 1. 3-Mesh Network Matrix Formulation & Cramer's Rule

For any planar electrical network with 3 independent loop currents, Kirchhoff's Voltage Law yields the matrix equation $\mathbf{R}\vec{I} = \vec{V}$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} R_{11} & -R_{12} & -R_{13} \\ -R_{21} & R_{22} & -R_{23} \\ -R_{31} & -R_{32} & R_{33} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I_1 \\ I_2 \\ I_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} V_1 \\ V_2 \\ V_3 \end{bmatrix}$$

In ANSI C, the 3x3 determinant of matrix $M$ is evaluated via cofactor expansion across row 0:

$$\Delta = M_{00}(M_{11}M_{22} - M_{12}M_{21}) - M_{01}(M_{10}M_{22} - M_{12}M_{20}) + M_{02}(M_{10}M_{21} - M_{11}M_{20})$$

Cramer's rule computes each current by substituting column $k$ with $\vec{V}$:

$$I_1 = \frac{\Delta_1}{\Delta}, \quad I_2 = \frac{\Delta_2}{\Delta}, \quad I_3 = \frac{\Delta_3}{\Delta}$$

⚡ 2. R-L-C Series Transient ODE Solvers: Forward Euler vs. RK4

The second-order differential equation for a series $R$-$L$-$C$ tank excited by step voltage $V_s$:

$$L \frac{d^2 v_C}{dt^2} + R \frac{d v_C}{dt} + \frac{1}{C} v_C = \frac{V_s}{C}$$

Decomposed into coupled first-order state variables $(v_C, i_L)$ in C:

$$\frac{dv_C}{dt} = \frac{i_L}{C}, \qquad \frac{di_L}{dt} = \frac{V_s - R \cdot i_L - v_C}{L}$$

Forward Euler (Explicit):

$$v_C^{(k+1)} = v_C^{(k)} + \Delta t \left(\frac{i_L^{(k)}}{C}\right), \quad i_L^{(k+1)} = i_L^{(k)} + \Delta t \left(\frac{V_s - R i_L^{(k)} - v_C^{(k)}}{L}\right)$$

Stability Criterion: Forward Euler requires $\Delta t \ll \frac{2}{\omega_n}$ where $\omega_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$. An overly large step size produces exponential divergence and artificial energy generation.

🖊 3. Fourth-Order Runge-Kutta (RK4) Integrator

RK4 samples four intermediate derivative slopes across the interval $[t, t + \Delta t]$ with global error $O(\Delta t^4)$:

$$\vec{k}_1 = \vec{f}(t, \vec{y}), \quad \vec{k}_2 = \vec{f}\left(t + \frac{\Delta t}{2}, \vec{y} + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\vec{k}_1\right)$$ $$\vec{k}_3 = \vec{f}\left(t + \frac{\Delta t}{2}, \vec{y} + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\vec{k}_2\right), \quad \vec{k}_4 = \vec{f}(t + \Delta t, \vec{y} + \Delta t \vec{k}_3)$$ $$\vec{y}^{(k+1)} = \vec{y}^{(k)} + \frac{\Delta t}{6}(\vec{k}_1 + 2\vec{k}_2 + 2\vec{k}_3 + \vec{k}_4)$$

This allows accurate simulation of under-damped ringing ($\zeta < 1$) without numerical dissipation or artificial growth.

🔄 4. AC Complex Phasor Arithmetic & Power Triangle

Representing complex impedances using user-defined C structures:

$$\mathbf{Z} = R + jX_L, \quad \mathbf{I} = \frac{\mathbf{V}}{\mathbf{Z}}, \quad \mathbf{S} = \mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{I}^* = P + jQ$$

Complex division formula implemented in C:

$$\frac{a + jb}{c + jd} = \frac{ac + bd}{c^2 + d^2} + j \frac{bc - ad}{c^2 + d^2}$$

Required shunt capacitance $C_{\mu\text{F}}$ to raise power factor from $\cos\phi_1$ to $\cos\phi_2$:

$$C_{\mu\text{F}} = \frac{P (\tan\phi_1 - \tan\phi_2)}{2 \pi f V^2} \times 10^6$$
