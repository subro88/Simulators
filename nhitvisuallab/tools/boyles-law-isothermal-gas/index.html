📖 Mathematical Theory & Thermodynamic Formulations

WBSCTE Applied Physics-I (BS103/BS107): Ideal Gas Law, Kinetic Theory, and Absolute Zero Extrapolation.

1. Ideal Gas Equation of State

The state of an ideal gas of $n$ moles is governed by:

$$P \cdot V = n \cdot R \cdot T$$

2. Individual Gas Laws Summary

  • Boyle's Law (Isothermal, $T=\text{const}$): $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \implies P \propto \frac{1}{V}$
  • Charles's Law (Isobaric, $P=\text{const}$): $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2} \implies V \propto T$
  • Gay-Lussac's Law (Isochoric, $V=\text{const}$): $\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2} \implies P \propto T$

3. Absolute Zero Extrapolation

Extrapolating $V$ vs. $T_{^\circ\text{C}}$ or $P$ vs. $T_{^\circ\text{C}}$ linearly back to $y=0$ gives the absolute zero temperature:

$$T_{\text{abs}} = -\frac{y_0}{m} = -273.15^\circ\text{C} = 0\text{ K}$$

📚 Comprehensive Experimental Manual & Guidelines

1. Objective & Physical Principles

To verify the fundamental Ideal Gas Laws: Boyle's Law ($PV=k_1$), Charles's Law ($V/T=k_2$), and Gay-Lussac's Law ($P/T=k_3$), observe microscopic hard-sphere particle kinetics, and extrapolate linear thermal expansion data to find Absolute Zero ($-273.15^\circ\text{C}$).

2. Step-by-Step Experimental Procedure
  1. Select Boyle's Law (Isothermal) mode. Adjust volume $V$ from $20\text{ mL}$ to $100\text{ mL}$ and record pressure $P$. Verify that $P \cdot V = \text{constant}$.
  2. Switch to Charles's Law (Isobaric) mode. Vary temperature $T$ from $-50^\circ\text{C}$ to $+150^\circ\text{C}$ using the slider or quick temperature bath buttons. Log data points into the observation table.
  3. Observe the linear regression line on the Operational Graph extending back to $V=0$ at $-273.15^\circ\text{C}$.
  4. Switch to Gay-Lussac's Law (Isochoric) mode and verify pressure rise with temperature at fixed volume.
  5. Export logged observations as CSV.
