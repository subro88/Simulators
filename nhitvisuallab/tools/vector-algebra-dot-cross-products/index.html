📖 Vector Calculus & Mechanics: Theory & Governing Equations BS101 Syllabus

1. The Scalar (Dot) Product

Given two 3D Euclidean vectors $\vec{A}, \vec{B} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, the scalar product is an algebraic operation returning a single real number:

$$\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = \|\vec{A}\| \|\vec{B}\| \cos\theta = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z$$

Geometric Projections:

  • Scalar Projection: $\text{proj}_{\vec{B}}(\vec{A}) = \|\vec{A}\| \cos\theta = \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{\|\vec{B}\|}$
  • Vector Projection: $\vec{A}_{\parallel} = \left(\frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{\|\vec{B}\|^2}\right)\vec{B}$
  • Orthogonal Rejection: $\vec{A}_{\perp} = \vec{A} - \vec{A}_{\parallel}$ where $\vec{A}_{\perp} \cdot \vec{B} = 0$.

Physical Application — Work Done:

Mechanical work is defined as the scalar product of force $\vec{F}$ and displacement $\vec{d}$: $$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = F d \cos\theta$$ By the Work-Energy Theorem, net work done equals the change in kinetic energy: $$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = \frac{1}{2} m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2} m v_i^2$$

2. The Vector (Cross) Product

The cross product of two 3D vectors produces a pseudovector perpendicular to both operands:

$$\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{vmatrix} = (A_y B_z - A_z B_y)\hat{i} + (A_z B_x - A_x B_z)\hat{j} + (A_x B_y - A_y B_x)\hat{k}$$

Geometric Invariant & Magnitude:

$$\|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}\| = \|\vec{A}\| \|\vec{B}\| \sin\theta = \text{Area of Parallelogram}$$

Anti-Commutative Property:

Reversing the order of operands reverses the resultant normal vector direction: $$\vec{B} \times \vec{A} = -(\vec{A} \times \vec{B})$$

Physical Application — Torque ($\vec{\tau}$):

The moment of a force applied at position $\vec{r}$ relative to a pivot is: $$\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} = r F \sin\theta\,\hat{n} = r_\perp F\,\hat{n}$$ governing angular acceleration by Newton's rotational law $\vec{\tau} = I \vec{\alpha}$.

1. Overview & Physical Principles

This virtual laboratory provides an interactive workbench for understanding vector algebra through its two foundational engineering applications:

  • Scalar (Dot) Product: Quantifies directional alignment between two vectors, modeling mechanical work done $W = \vec{F}\cdot\vec{d}$. The component perpendicular to displacement produces strictly zero work.
  • Vector (Cross) Product: Quantifies rotational tendency and spanned perpendicular area, modeling torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{r}\times\vec{F}$. The lever arm collinear with force produces zero moment.

5. Numerical Problem & Sample Calculation

Sample Problem: A force $\vec{F} = 30\hat{i} + 40\hat{j}\text{ N}$ acts on a body displacing it through $\vec{d} = 4\hat{i} + 3\hat{j}\text{ m}$. Calculate the work done, and find the torque if the force is applied at position $\vec{r} = 2\hat{i}\text{ m}$.

Solution:

  • $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = (30 \times 4) + (40 \times 3) = 120 + 120 = 240\text{ Joules}$.
  • $\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} = (2\hat{i}) \times (30\hat{i} + 40\hat{j}) = 2 \times 40 (\hat{i} \times \hat{j}) = 80\hat{k}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

Engineering Reference & Laboratory Standards: Vector Algebra Work & Torque Lab

Vector mathematics constitutes the mathematical bedrock of physical engineering. In mechanical, civil, and electrical domains, physical quantities are categorized into scalars (independent of coordinate orientation, such as mass, temperature, and work) and vectors (possessing both magnitude and spatial orientation, such as force, velocity, and torque).

Work-Energy Formulation via Scalar Products

When a mechanical force acts upon a constrained rigid body, the energy transferred is strictly governed by the collinear projection of the applied force along the displacement vector. The perpendicular component produces constraint forces (such as surface normal reaction) that do no mechanical work. The dot product $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}$ algebraically filters out these non-working orthogonal forces.

Moment of Force & Rotational Dynamics via Vector Products

In revolute kinematics and rigid body mechanics, torque represents the rotational efficacy of a force about a specific axis of revolution. The cross product $\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$ naturally incorporates both the effective perpendicular moment arm $r_\perp = r\sin\theta$ and the orthogonal rotational axis dictated by the right-hand screw rule.

Lagrange Identity & Algebraic Invariants

The fundamental geometric connection between scalar and vector products is encapsulated by the Lagrange Identity: $$\|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}\|^2 + (\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}\|^2 = \|\vec{A}\|^2 \|\vec{B}\|^2$$ This identity demonstrates that the squared area of the spanned parallelogram plus the squared scalar projection product exactly equals the product of their squared magnitudes, reflecting the trigonometric Pythagorean identity $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.
