01LVDT Induction & Demodulation

The differential secondary voltage encodes both magnitude and direction through its phase relative to the carrier:

  • At the null, an ideal LVDT reads 0 V; residual quadrature leakage leaves a few mV.
  • Raw RMS magnitude cannot distinguish +5 mm from −5 mm — PSD recovers the sign.

02PID Control Law

  • P reduces rise time but leaves offset on a Type-0 plant; I removes offset; D adds damping.
  • Anti-windup freezes integration at the saturation rails; derivative-on-measurement avoids setpoint kick.

Standard operating procedure

  1. Open the LVDT module and drag the core (or the vernier) through the ±15 mm stroke; watch the carrier phase flip on the DSO.
  2. Switch between raw AC and phase-sensitive demodulation to see how signed displacement is recovered.
  3. In the PID module, trigger a step test, tune Kp/Ki/Kd, and read tr, %OS, ts and ess from the metrics panel.
  4. Enable anti-windup and derivative-on-measurement, inject a disturbance, and inspect s-plane pole migration.
  5. Log readings, export the report, then attempt the nine tuning labs, practice calculations and quiz.
