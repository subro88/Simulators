📖 Governing Electrostatic & Structural Mechanics Equations

1. Euler-Bernoulli Beam Statics & Bending Stiffness ($k$)

For a prismatic, uniform horizontal beam clamped rigidly at $x = 0$ (encastre) and loaded with tip force $F = M g$ at $x = L$:

$$I_g = \frac{b d^3}{12} \quad (\text{Second Moment of Area for Rectangular Cross-Section})$$ $$\delta = \frac{M g L^3}{3 Y I_g} = \frac{4 M g L^3}{Y b d^3} \quad (\text{Maximum Tip Deflection})$$ $$k = \frac{F}{\delta} = \frac{3 Y I_g}{L^3} = \frac{Y b d^3}{4 L^3} \quad (\text{Effective Beam Spring Constant in N/m})$$

where $b$ is the beam breadth (width), $d$ is the thickness (depth), and $L$ is the vibrating cantilever length.

2. Rayleigh's Beam Effective Mass Correction ($m_{\text{eff}} = \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}}$)

A real cantilever beam has distributed mass $m_{\text{beam}} = \rho b d L$. Because elements near the fixed clamp move very little while elements near the tip oscillate with maximum velocity, Rayleigh's energy method calculates the equivalent lumped mass at the tip:

$$T_{\text{kinetic}} = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^L \rho b d \left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}\right)^2 dx = \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{eff}} \dot{y}_{\text{tip}}^2 \implies m_{\text{eff}} = \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \approx 0.2357 \cdot m_{\text{beam}}$$ $$M_{\text{total}} = M + m_{\text{eff}} = M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}}$$

Neglecting this correction leads to systematic overestimation of Young's Modulus, particularly at light loads $M$.

3. Natural Vibration Frequency ($f$) & Time Period ($T$) Linearization

The undamped natural angular frequency $\omega_n$ and oscillation period $T$ are given by:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{M + \frac{33}{140}m_{\text{beam}}}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{4 L^3 \left( M + \frac{33}{140}m_{\text{beam}} \right)}{Y b d^3}}$$ $$T^2 = \left( \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \right) M + \left( \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \cdot \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \right)$$

Plotting $T^2$ on the Y-axis against $M$ on the X-axis yields a straight line with slope $S_M = \frac{16\pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3}$. Rearranging yields:

$$\mathbf{Y = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{b d^3 \cdot S_M}} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Negative X-Intercept} = -\frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} = -m_{\text{eff}}$$
4. Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure & Calibrations
  1. Dimensions: Clamp the metal strip rigidly with length $L = 50.0\text{ cm}$. Measure width $b = 25.0\text{ mm}$ and thickness $d = 2.0\text{ mm}$.
  2. Mass Calibration: Suspend tip mass $M = 100\text{ g}$. Pluck the tip with a vertical displacement of $15\text{ mm}$ and release.
  3. Timing: Let the optical photogate record the time for 20 oscillations ($t_{20}$). Compute period $T = t_{20}/20$ and $T^2$.
  4. Sweep: Repeat for $M = 200, 300, 400, 500, 600\text{ g}$.
  5. Graph: Plot $T^2$ vs $M$. Extract the slope $S_M$ and negative X-intercept to determine $Y$ and $m_{\text{eff}}$.
