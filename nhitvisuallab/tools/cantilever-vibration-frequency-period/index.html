📖 Laboratory Manual: Cantilever Beam Vibration & Young's Modulus ($Y$)

1. Aim & Objective

To determine the Young's Modulus ($Y$) of the material of a uniform metallic cantilever blade by measuring its period of flexural oscillation ($T$) for various tip loads ($M$), to verify the linear relationship between $T^2$ and $M$, and to experimentally extract the Rayleigh effective mass ($m_{\text{eff}}$) of the vibrating beam.

2. Theoretical Principles

A cantilever is a horizontal beam rigidly clamped at one end and free at the other. When a load $M$ is applied at the free end, the beam bends downward under flexure. According to Euler-Bernoulli Beam Theory, the bending moment $M(x)$ at distance $x$ from the fixed clamp is balanced by the internal elastic restoring couple:

$$M(x) = Y \cdot I_g \cdot \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} = F \cdot (L - x)$$

where $Y$ is Young's modulus, $L$ is the vibrating free length, and $I_g$ is the geometrical moment of inertia of the cross-section. For a rectangular blade of breadth $b$ and thickness $d$:

$$I_g = \frac{b \cdot d^3}{12}$$

Static Deflection ($\delta$):

Integrating twice subject to the boundary conditions $y(0) = 0$ and $y'(0) = 0$ gives the deflection curve:

$$y(x) = \frac{F}{6 Y I_g} \left(3 L x^2 - x^3\right)$$

The maximum static deflection $\delta$ at the free tip ($x = L$) under load $F = M g$ is:

$$\delta = \frac{M g L^3}{3 Y I_g} = \frac{4 M g L^3}{Y b d^3}$$

Equivalent Spring Stiffness ($k$):

From Hooke's Law ($F = k \cdot \delta$), the transverse flexural stiffness is:

$$k = \frac{F}{\delta} = \frac{3 Y I_g}{L^3} = \frac{Y b d^3}{4 L^3}$$

Rayleigh's Effective Beam Mass Correction ($m_{\text{eff}}$):

The cantilever blade itself possesses distributed mass $m_{\text{beam}} = \rho b d L$. Because elements near the clamped end hardly oscillate while the tip moves with maximum velocity, only a fraction of the beam's mass participates dynamically. Using Rayleigh's Energy Method, equating kinetic energy yields:

$$m_{\text{eff}} = \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \approx 0.2357 \cdot m_{\text{beam}}$$

The total vibrating inertia is therefore $M_{\text{total}} = M + m_{\text{eff}}$.

Frequency and Period of Flexural Vibration:

The undamped equation of transverse motion is $(M + m_{\text{eff}}) \ddot{y} + k y = 0$. The natural angular frequency $\omega_n$ and time period $T$ are:

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{M + m_{\text{eff}}}}, \quad T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{M + m_{\text{eff}}}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{4 L^3 (M + m_{\text{eff}})}{Y b d^3}}$$

Squaring both sides yields the primary experimental equation:

$$T^2 = \left(\frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3}\right) M + \left(\frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3}\right) m_{\text{eff}}$$

A plot of $T^2$ on the Y-axis against added tip mass $M$ on the X-axis yields a straight line with slope $S_M$:

$$S_M = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \implies Y = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{b d^3 \cdot S_M}$$

The negative X-intercept of this straight line gives the Rayleigh effective mass: $\text{Intercept} = -m_{\text{eff}}$.

3. Apparatus Required
  • Uniform metallic cantilever blade (Mild Steel, Brass, or Aluminum) of length $\approx 100\text{ cm}$.
  • Heavy cast-iron bench G-clamp with wooden packing blocks for rigid clamping.
  • Lightweight tip weight hanger with a set of calibrated 50 g slotted dead-weights.
  • Digital stopwatch or automated infrared photogate timer (precision 0.01 s).
  • Vernier Calipers (for breadth $b$) and Slide Callipers / Screw Gauge (for thickness $d$).
  • Standard 100 cm meter scale.
4. Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure
  1. Measure the total mass $m_{\text{beam}}$ of the blade and its total length. Compute mass per unit length $\mu = m_{\text{beam}} / L_{\text{total}}$.
  2. Measure the breadth $b$ of the blade at 3 different points using Vernier Calipers and record the mean.
  3. Measure the thickness $d$ of the blade at 5 different locations using a Screw Gauge (taking zero-error into account) and calculate the mean thickness.
  4. Clamp one end of the blade firmly to the laboratory bench between two flat wooden blocks using the heavy G-clamp such that a chosen length $L$ (e.g. 60 cm) projects horizontally.
  5. Suspend the empty weight hanger from the free tip. Ensure that the clamping screw is tightened solidly so that no slipping occurs during oscillation.
  6. Depress the free end slightly downward (by no more than 2 to 3 cm to stay strictly within the elastic limit and small-angle linear regime) and release it gently to initiate vertical transverse oscillations.
  7. Verify that the beam oscillates purely in the vertical plane without twisting or lateral wobble.
  8. Using the photogate or stopwatch, start timing as the tip crosses the equilibrium position and record the time for 20 complete oscillations ($t_{20}$). Repeat twice for consistency and find the mean period $T = t_{20} / 20$.
  9. Add a 50 g slotted weight to the hanger, wait for steady oscillations, and record the new period $T$.
  10. Repeat the measurement for 5 to 6 increasing masses ($M = 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250\text{ g}$).
  11. Plot $T^2$ against $M$. Fit a linear regression line, determine the slope $S_M$ and the negative X-intercept ($-m_{\text{eff}}$), and calculate Young's Modulus $Y$.

6. Precautions & Sources of Error
  • Rigid Clamping: The fixed end must be clamped extremely tight between wooden jaws. Any bench vibration or clamp compliance acts as a series spring, lowering the measured frequency and underestimating $Y$.
  • Small Amplitude Oscillations: Keep tip deflection small ($\delta \ll L$) so that Euler-Bernoulli linear beam assumptions ($\frac{dy}{dx} \ll 1$) and Hooke's law hold true. Large amplitudes induce non-linear geometric stiffening.
  • Pure Vertical Mode: Ensure the tip is plucked straight downward without any lateral displacement to avoid coupling with torsional or lateral vibration modes.
  • Accurate Thickness Measurement: Since $d$ appears cubed ($d^3$) in the formula for $Y$, a 1% error in thickness $d$ produces a 3% error in the calculated Young's Modulus.
