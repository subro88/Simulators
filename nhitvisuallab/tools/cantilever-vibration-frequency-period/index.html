📖 Transverse Flexural Oscillations of an Elastic Cantilever Beam

A cantilever beam is a horizontal prismatic structural member clamped rigidly at one end ($x = 0$) and free at the other ($x = L$). When disturbed by a transverse tip displacement or loaded with an end mass $M$, it performs transverse flexural oscillations governed by elasticity, cross-sectional geometry, and distributed inertia.

1. Euler-Bernoulli Bending Stiffness & Statics

For a beam of rectangular cross-section with breadth $b$ and depth/thickness $d$, the second moment of area (area moment of inertia) about the neutral axis is:

$$I_g = \frac{b \cdot d^3}{12}$$

Under a concentrated static tip load $F = M g$, the static tip deflection $\delta$ and the effective elastic spring constant $k$ are derived from beam flexure theory:

$$\delta = \frac{M g L^3}{3 Y I_g} = \frac{4 M g L^3}{Y b d^3} \quad \implies \quad k = \frac{F}{\delta} = \frac{3 Y I_g}{L^3} = \frac{Y b d^3}{4 L^3} \quad (\text{N/m})$$

2. Distributed Inertia & Rayleigh's Beam Effective Mass Correction

A real laboratory cantilever blade has finite distributed mass $m_{\text{beam}} = \rho \cdot b \cdot d \cdot L$. Because points near the clamp move less than points near the tip, their kinetic energy is lower. Applying Rayleigh's energy quotient with the static deflection curve shape yields an equivalent concentrated tip mass:

$$m_{\text{eff}} = \frac{33}{140} \cdot m_{\text{beam}} \approx 0.2357 \cdot m_{\text{beam}}$$ $$M_{\text{total}} = M + m_{\text{eff}} = M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}}$$

3. Natural Frequency & Linearized Period Formulation

The angular natural frequency $\omega_n$ and oscillation time period $T$ become:

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{M_{\text{total}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{Y b d^3}{4 L^3 \left( M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \right)}}$$ $$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{4 L^3 \left( M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \right)}{Y b d^3}}$$

Squaring both sides linearizes the relationship with respect to the concentrated load $M$:

$$T^2 = \left( \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \right) M + \left( \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \cdot \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \right)$$
  • Slope $S_M$: $\mathbf{Y = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{b d^3 \cdot S_M}}$ (Direct experimental evaluation of Young's Modulus).
  • Negative X-Intercept: $-\frac{\text{Intercept}}{S_M} = -\frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} = -m_{\text{eff}}$ (Direct measurement of Rayleigh's effective mass).

4. Damped Oscillation Equation of Motion (RK4 Engine)

In the presence of air drag and internal viscoelastic damping ratio $\zeta$, the equation of motion is solved via 4th-order Runge-Kutta numerical integration:

$$\frac{d^2 y}{dt^2} + 2\zeta\omega_n \frac{dy}{dt} + \omega_n^2 y = 0$$ $$\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} \quad \text{and} \quad \Lambda = \ln\left( \frac{y_n}{y_{n+1}} \right) = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$$

📗 Standard Operating Procedure Manual (Euler-Bernoulli Flexural Scaling & Young's Modulus)
  1. Apparatus Setup: Select specimen material (e.g., Mild Steel). Verify beam cross-section: breadth $b = 25.0\text{ mm}$ and thickness $d = 2.0\text{ mm}$. Set clamping length $L = 50.0\text{ cm}$.
  2. Theoretical Calculation: Compute theoretical beam mass $m_{\text{beam}} = \rho \cdot b \cdot d \cdot L$ and Rayleigh's effective mass $m_{\text{eff}} = \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}}$.
  3. Tip Mass Variation ($T^2 \propto M$): Attach tip load $M = 100\text{ g}$. Pluck tip with $15-20\text{ mm}$ initial deflection. Click "Start Multi-Cycle Timer" to record time for 20 complete oscillations ($t_{20}$). Compute mean period $T = t_{20}/20$ and $T^2$. Repeat for $M = 200, 300, 400, 500, 600\text{ g}$.
  4. Length Variation ($T^2 \propto L^3$): Lock tip load at $M = 200\text{ g}$. Vary clamping position $L = 40, 50, 60, 70, 80\text{ cm}$ and verify cubic scaling $T \propto L^{3/2}$.
  5. Young's Modulus Extraction: Plot $T^2$ vs $M$, calculate slope $S_M = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3}$, and extract Young's Modulus: $Y = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{b d^3 S_M}$. Confirm the negative X-intercept matches theoretical Rayleigh mass $-m_{\text{eff}}$.
