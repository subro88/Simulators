📖 Comprehensive Theory, Structural Mechanics & Syllabus Reference

1. Euler-Bernoulli Beam Bending & Cantilever Stiffness ($k$)

An ideal cantilever is a horizontal prismatic elastic beam rigidly clamped at one end ($x=0$, encastre) and free at the other ($x=L$). Under an applied transverse downward concentrated load $F = Mg$ at the tip, the differential equation governing the neutral elastic curvature is given by Euler-Bernoulli beam theory:

$$Y I_g \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} = M_{\text{bending}}(x) = F (L - x)$$

Integrating twice with fixed boundary conditions $y(0) = 0$ and $\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{x=0} = 0$ yields the deflection profile:

$$y(x) = \frac{M g}{6 Y I_g} \left( 3 L x^2 - x^3 \right)$$

For a rectangular cross-section of breadth $b$ and depth/thickness $d$, the second moment of area about the neutral axis is $I_g = \frac{b d^3}{12}$. Evaluating maximum tip deflection $\delta = y(L)$:

$$\delta = \frac{M g L^3}{3 Y I_g} = \frac{4 M g L^3}{Y b d^3} \implies k = \frac{F}{\delta} = \frac{Y b d^3}{4 L^3} \quad (\text{N/m})$$
2. Rayleigh's Distributed Mass Correction & Effective Tip Mass ($m_{\text{eff}}$)

A physical metal blade has a distributed mass $m_{\text{beam}} = \rho b d L$. Because sections near the clamp remain nearly stationary while sections near the tip oscillate at full amplitude, the distributed mass cannot be simply added directly to $M$.

Using Lord Rayleigh's kinetic energy equivalence method, the total kinetic energy of the vibrating continuous beam is equated to an equivalent lumped mass $m_{\text{eff}}$ concentrated at the free tip:

$$T_{\text{kinetic}} = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^L \left(\frac{m_{\text{beam}}}{L}\right) \dot{y}^2(x) \, dx = \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{eff}} \dot{y}_{\text{tip}}^2$$

Substituting the Euler-Bernoulli static shape mode $y(x) = y_{\text{tip}} \left( \frac{3 L x^2 - x^3}{2 L^3} \right)$ and evaluating the definite integral yields the exact Rayleigh factor:

$$m_{\text{eff}} = \frac{33}{140} \cdot m_{\text{beam}} \approx 0.235714 \cdot m_{\text{beam}}$$

The total effective vibrating mass of the cantilever oscillator is therefore $M_{\text{total}} = M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}}$.

3. Frequency ($f$), Period ($T$), and Linearized Determination of Young's Modulus ($Y$)

The angular natural frequency of transverse flexural oscillation is:

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{M_{\text{total}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{Y b d^3}{4 L^3 \left( M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \right)}}$$

The natural period of oscillation $T$ is:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}}}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{4 L^3 \left( M + \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \right)}{Y b d^3}}$$

Squaring both sides linearizes the relationship with respect to the concentrated tip load $M$:

$$T^2 = \left( \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \right) M + \left( \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \cdot \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} \right)$$

A plot of $T^2$ on the vertical axis against $M$ on the horizontal axis yields a straight line with slope $S_M$ and negative X-intercept:

$$\text{Slope } S_M = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{Y b d^3} \implies \mathbf{Y = \frac{16 \pi^2 L^3}{b d^3 S_M}}$$ $$\text{Negative X-Intercept: } -M_{\text{intercept}} = \frac{33}{140} m_{\text{beam}} = m_{\text{eff}}$$
4. Damped Oscillation Equation & Runge-Kutta 4th Order (RK4) Solution

The real physical system experiences both atmospheric drag and internal viscoelastic hysteresis, modeled as a 2nd-order damped harmonic oscillator:

$$\frac{d^2 y}{dt^2} + 2 \zeta \omega_n \frac{dy}{dt} + \omega_n^2 y = 0$$

Where $\zeta < 1.0$ is the dimensionless damping ratio. The damped circular frequency is $\omega_d = \omega_n \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$, and consecutive peak displacement amplitudes decay logarithmically:

$$\Lambda = \ln\left( \frac{y_n}{y_{n+1}} \right) = \frac{2\pi \zeta}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$$

Our simulation integrates this equation continuously using a 4th-Order Runge-Kutta (RK4) numerical scheme with sub-millisecond timesteps ($\Delta t = 0.001\text{ s}$) to prevent numerical energy divergence.
