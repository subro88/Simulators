🔥 Closed-Cup Flash & Fire Point Thermodynamics (IP 33 / ISO 13736)

The Flash Point of a flammable liquid is defined as the lowest temperature at which its equilibrium saturated vapor pressure produces a vapor-air concentration above the liquid surface equal to the Lower Flammability Limit (LFL).

1. Antoine Vapor Pressure & Flammability Envelope

The saturated vapor pressure $P_{\text{vap}}(T)$ of a volatile distillate is modeled by the empirical Antoine equation:

$$\log_{10} P_{\text{vap}}(T) = A - \frac{B}{T_{^\circ\text{C}} + C}$$ $$y_{\text{fuel}}(T) = \frac{P_{\text{vap}}(T)}{P_{\text{baro}}}$$

Ignition occurs during test flame dipping if and only if: $$y_{\text{LFL}} \le y_{\text{fuel}}(T) \le y_{\text{UFL}}$$

2. Fire Point Dynamics (Sustained Evaporation)

The Fire Point ($T_{\text{fire}}$) is determined by holding the slide shutter fully open (or uncovering the cup) and continuing heat input. It represents the temperature where the liquid surface evaporation rate ($\dot{m}_{\text{evap}}$) equals or exceeds the burning rate ($\dot{m}_{\text{burn}}$), sustaining continuous combustion for at least 5 seconds.

$$T_{\text{fire}} \approx T_{\text{flash}} + \Delta T_{\text{fire}} \quad (\text{Typically } 3^\circ\text{C} \text{ to } 8^\circ\text{C} \text{ above Flash Point})$$

3. Atmospheric Barometric Pressure Normalization

Because vapor pressure equilibrium depends on surrounding atmospheric pressure, all observed test readings ($T_{\text{obs}}$) must be corrected to standard sea-level pressure ($101.325\text{ kPa} = 760\text{ mmHg}$):

$$\text{In kPa: } \quad T_{\text{corrected}} = T_{\text{obs}} + 0.25 \cdot (101.3 - P_{\text{baro}})$$ $$\text{In mmHg: } \quad T_{\text{corrected}} = T_{\text{obs}} + 0.033 \cdot (760 - P_{\text{baro}})$$

4. IP 33 Heating & Mechanical Stirring Rigor

  • Heating Rate: Maintained at a strict uniform rate of $1.0^\circ\text{C} \pm 0.2^\circ\text{C}$ per minute.
  • Air Jacket Buffering: The annular air gap between the outer heating water bath and inner brass cup prevents localized liquid hotspot boiling.
  • Stirrer Cadence: Stirred continuously at 1 to 2 revolutions per second, but strictly halted during the 3-second test flame dip.

📗 Standard Operating Procedure Manual (IP 33 / ISO 13736)
  1. Sample Preparation: Cool or pre-condition the petroleum specimen to at least 10°C below expected flash point before filling the 50 mL brass cup to the internal meniscus fill mark.
  2. Bath Preheating: Fill outer bath with water (preheated to ~54°C for kerosene/jet fuel) or ice/glycol for sub-zero samples.
  3. Controlled Heating & Stirring: Adjust heater to maintain $1.0^\circ\text{C}/\text{min}$ temperature rise. Stir smoothly at 1-2 rev/s.
  4. Dip Testing Cadence: At every 0.5°C temperature rise, cease stirring, actuate slide shutter (1s dip down, 1s hold, 1s close), and resume stirring.
  5. Flash Point Detection: Record $T_{\text{obs}}$ when a distinct electric-blue deflagration pop spreads across the closed headspace. Apply barometric pressure correction.
  6. Fire Point Determination: Remove lid or lock shutter open, continue heating, and record temperature where surface flame burns continuously for $\ge 5\text{ seconds}$.
