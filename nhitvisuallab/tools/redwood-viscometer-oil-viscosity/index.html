📖 Viscometry Standards Theoretical Principles

1. Redwood No. 1 Viscometer (British Standard IP 70)

Measures the efflux time $t_R$ of $50.0\text{ mL}$ of oil through an agate jet ($D = 1.62\text{ mm}$, $L = 10.0\text{ mm}$). Kinematic viscosity $\nu$ (in $\text{cSt}$):

$$\nu = 0.260 \cdot t_R - \frac{179}{t_R} \quad (34\text{ s} \le t_R \le 100\text{ s}), \qquad \nu = 0.247 \cdot t_R - \frac{50}{t_R} \quad (t_R > 100\text{ s})$$

2. Saybolt Universal Viscometer (American Standard ASTM D88)

Measures efflux time $t_S$ of $60.0\text{ mL}$ through a hardened stainless steel tip ($D = 1.765\text{ mm}$, $L = 12.25\text{ mm}$):

$$\nu = 0.226 \cdot t_S - \frac{195}{t_S} \quad (32\text{ s} \le t_S \le 100\text{ s}), \qquad \nu = 0.220 \cdot t_S - \frac{135}{t_S} \quad (t_S > 100\text{ s})$$

3. Engler Viscometer (German Standard DIN 51560)

$$^\circ\text{E} = \frac{t_{E,\text{oil}} (200\text{ mL})}{W_F (200\text{ mL water at } 20^\circ\text{C})}, \qquad \nu = 7.60 \cdot (^\circ\text{E}) - \frac{4.00}{^\circ\text{E}}$$
