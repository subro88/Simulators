Straight Line

Both wheels travel the same distance, so both turn at the carrier speed. The spider gears do not rotate on their own axis — they merely orbit with the carrier.

  • Left = Right = Carrier
  • Spider spin = 0

Turning (Left / Right)

The outer wheel must cover a larger radius than the inner wheel. The differential splits speed: outer faster, inner slower, always summing to twice the carrier.

  • Outer + Inner = 2 × Carrier
  • Spider gears spin to allow the difference

Wheel Slip

If one wheel loses traction (ice, mud), it can spin at up to 2 × carrier while the gripping wheel stops. Open differentials send torque to the fastest-spinning wheel — the classic "one wheel spins" problem.

Jacked Up

With one wheel off the ground (zero resistance), that wheel turns at 2 × carrier and the grounded wheel is stationary. A perfect demonstration of the speed-splitting law.

  • One wheel = 0, other = 2 × Carrier

The Governing Law

For any open differential:

  • ωleft + ωright = 2 × ωcarrier
  • ωspider = (ωouter − ωcarrier) × (Taxle / Tspider)

About the Automotive Differential

A differential is the gear train that lets the driven wheels of a vehicle rotate at different speeds while receiving power from one driveshaft. Its three jobs are to (1) turn the drive torque 90° from the longitudinal propeller shaft to the transverse axle, (2) provide the final drive reduction via the crown wheel and pinion, and (3) split that rotation between the left and right wheels so they can cover different distances in a turn.

This simulator blends a classic 2D plan-view of the axle with an interactive 3D bevel-gear model built in the style of ProCalcLab's 3D differential. Adjust the carrier (drive) RPM, choose Straight, Turn, Slip or Jacked, set the turn bias, spider count and gear teeth, and watch both the law ωleft + ωright = 2ωcarrier hold, and the spider gears spin only when the wheels differ.
