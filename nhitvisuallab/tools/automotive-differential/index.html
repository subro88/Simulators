📖 Mechanical Principles & Mathematical Derivations Syllabus Reference ME501/AT601

1. Epicyclic Bevel-Gear Kinematics

An automotive open differential is an epicyclic bevel gear train comprising: a carrier/case (driven by the ring gear at angular velocity \(\omega_c\)), two side (sun) gears splined to the left and right half-shafts (\(\omega_L\), \(\omega_R\)), and spider (planet) bevel gears mounted on a cross-pin inside the carrier.

$$\text{Fundamental Kinematic Identity:} \quad \omega_c = \frac{\omega_L + \omega_R}{2}$$ $$\Longleftrightarrow \quad \omega_L + \omega_R = 2\omega_c$$

This identity holds regardless of the individual wheel speeds. The carrier always rotates at the arithmetic mean of the two wheel speeds. In straight driving, \(\omega_L = \omega_R = \omega_c\) and the spider gears do not rotate on their own axis — they merely orbit with the carrier.

2. Torque Distribution & Equal Split

In an open differential, both side gears mesh with the same spider gears. By Newton's Third Law applied to the bevel gear mesh, the reaction torques on both side gears are equal:

$$T_L = T_R = \frac{T_c}{2}$$

This equal torque split is the fundamental limitation of an open differential: if one wheel loses traction (e.g., on ice), the maximum torque deliverable to both wheels equals twice the traction limit of the slipping wheel — effectively zero useful traction.

3. Spider Gear Self-Spin

When the wheels rotate at different speeds (turning), the spider gears must spin on their own cross-pin axis to accommodate the speed difference:

$$\omega_{\text{spider}} = (\omega_{\text{outer}} - \omega_c) \times \frac{T_{\text{axle}}}{T_{\text{spider}}}$$

Where \(T_{\text{axle}}\) and \(T_{\text{spider}}\) are the tooth counts of the side gear and spider gear respectively. On a straight road, \(\omega_{\text{outer}} = \omega_c\), so the spider self-spin is zero.

4. Limited-Slip (LSD) & Locked Spool

A Limited-Slip Differential adds friction clutches or viscous coupling that resist the relative rotation of the side gears. When one wheel starts spinning faster, the clutch pack transfers additional torque to the slower (gripping) wheel:

$$T_{\text{slow}} = \frac{T_c}{2} + T_{\text{bias}}, \qquad T_{\text{fast}} = \frac{T_c}{2} - T_{\text{bias}}$$

A Spool (Locked Differential) welds or locks both axles together: \(\omega_L = \omega_R = \omega_c\) always, regardless of traction conditions. Maximum traction but causes tire scrub in turns and is used only for off-road or drag racing.

5. Final Drive Ratio

$$\text{Final Drive Ratio} = \frac{T_{\text{ring}}}{T_{\text{pinion}}}$$

Typical values: Highway 2.73–3.08, Balanced 3.23–3.55, Performance 3.73–4.10, Towing 4.30–4.88.

📚 Comprehensive User Guide & Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
1. Apparatus Overview & Mechanical Principle
An automotive open differential is an epicyclic bevel-gear mechanism that allows the driven wheels of a vehicle to rotate at different speeds while receiving power from a single driveshaft. It performs three functions: (1) turns the drive torque 90° from the propeller shaft to the transverse axle via the ring-and-pinion, (2) provides the final drive gear reduction, and (3) splits rotation between left and right wheels via spider planet bevel gears so they can cover different arc distances in turns.
2. Experimental Protocol & Workflow Steps
  1. Set up the gear train by adjusting axle gear teeth, spider gear teeth, ring gear, and pinion teeth.
  2. Select Straight mode: verify \(\omega_L = \omega_R = \omega_c\) and spider self-spin = 0.
  3. Switch to Left Turn or Right Turn: adjust Turn Bias slider and verify \(\omega_L + \omega_R = 2\omega_c\) holds at every bias setting.
  4. Select Slip mode: observe one wheel at \(2\omega_c\) and the other at 0. Understand the open differential limitation.
  5. Toggle LSD and Spool modes: compare behavior under the same maneuver and observe torque redistribution.
  6. Click Auto Sweep to automatically log readings across the full turn bias range.
3. Interpreting the Analytical Graphs
  • Tab 1 (Wheel Speeds): Shows \(\omega_L\) and \(\omega_R\) across turn bias. Their sum always equals \(2\omega_c\) (green reference line).
  • Tab 2 (Spider Spin): Spider self-spin increases proportionally with the wheel speed difference.
  • Tab 3 (Torque Split): Open shows flat 50:50; LSD shows biased distribution; Spool shows locked equal.
  • Tab 4 (Speed Ratio): \(\omega_L/\omega_R\) ratio vs turn angle — diverges from 1.0 during turns, extreme at slip conditions.
