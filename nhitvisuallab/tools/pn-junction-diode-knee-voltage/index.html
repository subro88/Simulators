📖 Mathematical Engine & Solid-State Physics Formulations

A P-N junction diode is a two-terminal semiconductor device that exhibits asymmetric nonlinear conduction. In forward bias, current increases exponentially once the applied voltage overcomes the built-in contact potential barrier; in reverse bias, only a minuscule saturation leakage flows until breakdown is reached.

1. The Shockley Diode Equation:
$$I_D = I_s \left( \exp\left( \frac{e V_D}{\eta k_B T} \right) - 1 \right) = I_s \left( \exp\left( \frac{V_D}{\eta V_T} \right) - 1 \right)$$ where $V_T = \frac{k_B T}{e} \approx 25.85\text{ mV}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$, $\eta$ is ideality factor (1.85 for Si, 1.05 for Ge), and $I_s$ is reverse saturation current.
2. Space-Charge Depletion Layer Width ($W$):
$$W(V) = W_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{V_D}{V_{bi}}}$$ where $V_{bi} \approx 0.70\text{ V}$ for Silicon and $0.30\text{ V}$ for Germanium. Under forward bias ($V_D > 0$), $W \to 0$, lowering the potential hill and permitting massive carrier diffusion.
3. Static ($R_{\text{dc}}$) and Dynamic ($r_{\text{ac}}$) Resistance:
Static (DC) Resistance: $$R_{\text{dc}} = \frac{V_D}{I_D}$$ Dynamic (AC / Incremental) Resistance: $$r_{\text{ac}} = \frac{\Delta V_D}{\Delta I_D} = \frac{1}{\left(\frac{dI_D}{dV_D}\right)} \approx \frac{\eta V_T}{I_D} + R_{\text{bulk}}$$

📚 Experimental Manual & Standard Operating Procedure

1. Aim of the Experiment & Apparatus Required

Aim: To draw the forward and reverse bias $V-I$ characteristics of a given P-N junction diode, determine its cut-in / knee voltage ($V_k$), and calculate its static and dynamic forward resistances.

Apparatus: Silicon (1N4007) and Germanium (1N34A) diodes, DC variable power supply ($0-3\text{ V}$ forward, $0-50\text{ V}$ reverse), current-limiting resistor ($220\ \Omega$), digital DC voltmeter, digital milliammeter/microammeter, polarity reversing commutator, breadboard, patch cords.

2. Step-by-Step Procedure: Forward Bias & Knee Voltage
  1. Select Silicon (1N4007). Set bias mode to Forward Bias (P-anode to positive, N-cathode to negative).
  2. Ensure the potentiometer starts at $0.00\text{ V}$.
  3. Gradually increase supply voltage in steps of $0.05\text{ V}$ up to $0.60\text{ V}$; observe that current remains under $0.5\text{ mA}$.
  4. Near $0.65\text{ V} - 0.70\text{ V}$, take fine steps of $0.01\text{ V}$. Observe the sharp upward knee bend as current surges above $5\text{ mA}$.
  5. Click Auto-Sweep Curve or record points into Table A. Check the tangent construction line intersecting the voltage axis at $V_k \approx 0.70\text{ V}$.
  6. Pick two points in the linear conduction region ($I_1 = 10\text{ mA}, I_2 = 20\text{ mA}$) to calculate dynamic resistance $r_{\text{ac}} = \frac{V_2 - V_1}{I_2 - I_1}$.
3. Step-by-Step Procedure: Reverse Bias & Breakdown
  1. Toggle the commutator to Reverse Bias mode. The meters auto-switch to $0-50\text{ V}$ and $0-100\ \mu\text{A}$.
  2. Sweep reverse voltage from $0\text{ V}$ to $30\text{ V}$. For Silicon, current remains practically zero ($\approx 1\text{ nA}$); for Germanium, note measurable leakage ($\approx 2-5\ \mu\text{A}$).
  3. Switch to Zener Diode preset and observe sharp reverse breakdown at $V_{BR} = 5.1\text{ V}$.
4. Precautions & Sources of Error
  • Current-Limiting Resistor: Never connect the diode directly across the DC supply without $R_{\text{limit}}$, as forward current can exceed maximum dissipation ratings.
  • Thermal Heating: Prolonged operation at currents $> 40\text{ mA}$ causes junction heating, shifting the knee voltage downward by $-2.0\text{ mV/}^\circ\text{C}$.
  • Zero Error: Ensure digital meters are zero-calibrated prior to sweep initiation.
