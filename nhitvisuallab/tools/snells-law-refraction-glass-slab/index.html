📖 Laboratory Manual: Determination of Refractive Index of Glass Slab

1. Aim & Objective

To trace the path of a ray of light through a rectangular glass slab for different angles of incidence, measure the angle of incidence ($\angle i$), angle of refraction ($\angle r$), and angle of emergence ($\angle e$), verify Snell's law of refraction, and determine the refractive index ($\mu$) and lateral displacement ($d$) of the glass slab.

2. Theoretical Principles

When a monochromatic ray of light travels from an optically rarer medium (air, $n_1 \approx 1$) into an optically denser medium (glass, $n_2 = \mu$), it bends towards the normal line. According to Snell's Law:

$$\mu = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r}$$

At the second refracting surface (glass to air), light travels from denser to rarer medium and bends away from the normal with emergent angle $e$. Since both refracting surfaces are strictly parallel, the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray ($\angle e = \angle i$).

Lateral Displacement ($d$):

The perpendicular distance between the incident ray produced forward and the emergent ray is known as Lateral Shift or Lateral Displacement ($d$). It is given by:

$$d = \frac{t \cdot \sin(i - r)}{\cos r}$$

Where $t$ is the thickness of the glass slab, $i$ is the angle of incidence, and $r$ is the angle of refraction.

3. Apparatus Required
  • Rectangular Glass Slab with smooth polished parallel faces.
  • Soft wooden drawing board with 4 brass thumb pins.
  • White drawing sheet (millimeter grid paper).
  • Four sharp steel fixation pins ($P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4$).
  • 360° / 180° Protractor, metric ruler, and sharp pencil.
4. Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure
  1. Fix a white paper sheet on the soft drawing board with thumb pins at the four corners.
  2. Place the glass slab centrally and draw its sharp boundary $ABCD$ with a fine pencil.
  3. Mark point $O$ roughly at the middle of face $AB$ and construct normal $N_1ON_2$.
  4. Draw an incident ray $IO$ inclined at chosen angle $i$ ($30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ$).
  5. Fix two pins $P_1$ and $P_2$ vertically on the incident ray at least $5\text{ cm}$ apart.
  6. Look through the opposite face $CD$ with one eye closed. Fix pin $P_3$ such that its foot lies in line with the virtual refracted images of $P_1$ and $P_2$.
  7. Fix pin $P_4$ such that $P_4, P_3$ and images of $P_1, P_2$ appear in a single straight line.
  8. Remove the slab and pins; mark pin pricks as $P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4$. Connect $P_4P_3$ to meet face $CD$ at $O'$.
  9. Join $O$ to $O'$ to represent the internal refracted ray. Measure $\angle r$, $\angle e$, and lateral shift $d$.
5. Standard Precautions & Sources of Error
  • Pin Spacing: Keep distance between $P_1-P_2$ and $P_3-P_4$ greater than $5\text{ cm}$ to avoid parallax error in slope alignment.
  • Vertical Fixation: Pins must be planted strictly perpendicular to the drawing sheet.
  • Base Alignment: Always align the feet / tips of the pins rather than the pinheads.
  • Pencil Sharpness: Use a $2\text{H}$ sharp pencil to minimize line thickness errors.
