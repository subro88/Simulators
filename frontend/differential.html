1. Drive Pinion & Crown Wheel

Transfers longitudinal propeller shaft drive 90° to the transverse axle shaft while providing final drive torque multiplication (4:1 reduction ratio).

2. Differential Carrier Housing

Rigid cast-steel cage bolted directly to the crown wheel. Rotates at speed Ncrown and carries the internal cross-pin holding the spider pinions.

3. Spider Planet Pinions

Bevel pinions mounted on the carrier cross-pin. During straight driving they don't spin on their pins. In turns, they spin to subtract speed from the inner wheel and add it to the outer wheel.

4. Axle Sun Gears

Bevel side gears splined directly to the left and right drive half-shafts that power the road wheels.

Guided Experiment: Kinematic Speed Equation Verification

1. Set the Driveshaft Input RPM slider to 2400 RPM (Crown Wheel speed = 600 RPM).
2. Select the Left Turn maneuver condition with 75% Outer Wheel Bias.
3. Observe the inner left wheel speed drops to 300 RPM while the outer right wheel speed rises to 900 RPM.
4. Verify the kinematic equation holds true: (300 + 900) / 2 = 600 RPM!

Question: What happens to the spider planet pinions when a vehicle turns left?

  • ✓ They rotate on their cross-pin, speeding up the outer right wheel while slowing the inner left wheel.
  • ✗ They lock completely and disconnect drive from the right axle shaft.
  • ✗ They disengage from the crown wheel.

How an Automotive Open Differential Works

An Automotive Differential is a planetary bevel-gear mechanism designed to allow the left and right driven road wheels to rotate at different angular velocities when turning, while continuing to transmit torque from the engine driveshaft.

The Fundamental Differential Kinematic Equation

Because the spider planet pinions mesh with both axle sun gears simultaneously, any decrease in the inner wheel speed results in an identical increase in the outer wheel speed:

Nleft + Nright = 2 × Ncrown
