1. Engine Flywheel

Heavy cast-iron disc bolted to the engine crankshaft. Serves as the primary drive friction surface and stores rotational inertia.

2. Friction Disc (Clutch Plate)

Splined to the gearbox input shaft. Uses high-friction organic or ceramic linings with internal torsional damping springs to smooth engagement shock.

3. Pressure Plate & Cover

Spring-loaded iron plate that clamps the friction disc against the flywheel face with several thousand Newtons of axial force.

4. Diaphragm Spring

Conical Belleville spring that acts as both clamping spring and release lever. Depressing the pedal pushes the release bearing against the inner fingers, relieving clamp force.

Guided Calculation Experiment: Torque Capacity vs Clamp Force

1. Set Clutch Type to Single-Plate (2 contact surfaces, $\mu = 0.35$, $R_m = 100\text{ mm}$).
2. Adjust Spring Clamp Force ($W$) to 4000 N.
3. Verify Torque Capacity: $T = 2 \times 0.35 \times 4000\text{ N} \times 0.10\text{ m} = \mathbf{280.0\text{ N·m}}$.
4. Now switch to Multi-Plate ($n=8$ active surfaces). Observe capacity quadruples to 1120.0 N·m!

Question: Why are multi-plate friction clutches used in motorcycles?

  • ✓ They provide high torque capacity in a small diameter by increasing the number of friction surfaces ($n$).
  • ✗ They eliminate the need for friction linings.
  • ✗ They only work with automatic transmissions.

2 Design Theories: Uniform Pressure vs. Uniform Wear
Uniform Pressure Theory assumes constant contact pressure across the disc face (applies to brand-new clutches). Uniform Wear Theory assumes wear is uniform over time (applies to used/worn clutches) and yields a slightly conservative mean radius $R_m = (R_o + R_i) / 2$.

How Automotive Friction Clutches Work

An Automotive Friction Clutch is the mechanical interface between the engine flywheel and gearbox input shaft. It enables smooth starting from rest and disengagement for gear changing.

Friction Torque Capacity Formula

Tcap = n × μ × W × Rm

Where n is the number of active friction contact surfaces, μ is the coefficient of friction, W is the spring clamp force (N), and Rm is the effective mean friction radius.
